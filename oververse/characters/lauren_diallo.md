# Lauren D'Iallo

**World:** Iolanu | **Gender:** Female | **Race:** Human  
**Status:** Sketch  
**Role:** Heiress to the D'Iallo expedition fortune. Hasn't picked her own lane yet — heir, explorer, something nobody in the family has tried — but nobody who's spent five minutes with her doubts she's headed for something enormous.

**Backstory:** Since McKinley D'Iallo left behind a diary that's still generating expeditions hundreds of years later, the family fortune has run on one expensive habit: chasing things nobody can prove exist. Cartographers on retainer, translators for dead languages, funding for digs that come back empty four times out of five and once in a while don't. Outsiders assume it's an eccentricity the family can afford. The family has never once called it that. They call it unfinished business.

Lauren wasn't born into it. She was ten weeks old when Odile D'Iallo — last of the line without an heir — took her in, not looking for something to rescue, just looking. What she found was a baby who apparently had opinions about everything from inside the crib. There's a family story, told constantly and without a hint of embarrassment, about the day seven-year-old Lauren informed the entire household that they were lucky to have her. Nobody corrected her. Nobody has since.

She's also, constantly, the first person in a room to notice who's being overlooked. Ignored the newest scullery girl for a week straight? Lauren already knows her name, her sister's name, and has decided — out loud, in front of everyone — that she's family now. She gives generously and without keeping score, easy to miss underneath the volume because she doesn't perform it, she just does it and moves on to the next opinion. But the generosity is on her terms — she decides how and when, never miserly, never negotiated. The deal is simple and telegraphed the same way every time: don't cross her, and things are perfectly fine. Cross her, or cross someone she's claimed, and there's no warning shot, because the terms themselves already were one.

She's the same generation as Gabby D'Olivier and Jasin Lochnivar's hunt for the missing 7th Godshard — and as far as anyone's told her, she has no idea her family name is currently being chased across half a continent by two strangers following her ancestor's handwriting.

Failure doesn't register with Lauren the way it does for most people — not because she spins a story to convince anyone else, but because on some level she genuinely believes it. Something else was the cause: bad luck, someone's incompetence, a plan undone by circumstances that weren't hers to own. She's not being cynical about it — she means it, completely, in the moment. What she will do, with real ingenuity, is find every possible route around whatever corrective action follows, not to get away with something she knows she did, but because she doesn't believe the correction was actually warranted in the first place. Learning from any of it tends to happen sideways: she dodges and argues and maneuvers around the lesson so thoroughly, for so long, that she eventually stumbles into the corrected behavior almost by accident, without ever consciously deciding to change — and she'll swear up and down it was her idea the whole time. None of this makes her any less generous, any less fierce about the people she loves. It's the same bedrock certainty that makes her worth having on your side, just aimed, for once, somewhere it doesn't actually serve her.

**Voice:** Blunt, unhedged, doesn't do modest — but the warmth is just as real as the edge.
- *"Don't cross me. We'll be perfectly fine."*
- *"Sit here. No, you. I said sit here."* — to whoever just got left out of something
- *"You're welcome, by the way. For all of this."* — said with a grin, not a sneer; it lands as a joke because everyone in earshot knows she means it a little
- *"I didn't ask for a warning shot. Don't give me one either."*
- *"Careful isn't a family trait. Never has been."*

**Tell:** The volume isn't the tell — that's just Lauren, all the time. The tell is when she goes quiet. The instant something crosses from *opinion* to *someone I love just got hurt*, everything drops — voice flat, fast, no heat left in it at all. Loud Lauren is Tuesday. Quiet Lauren is the one people get out of the way of.
