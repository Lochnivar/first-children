# Lucas Higgs, Bosun

**World:** Iolanu | **Gender:** Male | **Race:** Human  
**Status:** Sketch  
**Role:** Bosun — runs deck crew through hands, not orders; earns loyalty by already knowing the work cold and being first at the problem, not loudest about it.

**Appearance:** *(open)*

**Backstory:** Found as an infant lashed to wreckage after a merchant vessel went down in a storm — no survivors claimed, no name that came with him, a head wound from the wreck that kept him drifting in and out for days. The crew that pulled him out didn't have anywhere to send him, so they didn't. He grew up ship's family, blood to no one aboard and kin to all of them. Speech came late and hard-won, and something else came out of that same injury that never fully resolved: for most people, knowing something and trusting that you know it happen almost as the same step. For Lucas, they don't. He can feel that he's right about a line, a knot, a heading — and still not trust it enough to just say so and move on, because the part of the process that's supposed to hand him that certainty for free never quite grew back. So he checks. Out loud, about things he's already right about. It reads as insecurity, and some of it is — he wants to be told he did well, wants it more than someone this competent should need to — but underneath it, it was never really about doubting his hands. It's that the loop everyone else runs silently, he has to run with a witness.

The crew that raised him figured this out early and never once made him feel small for it. Someone always answered. That's most of why he grew up warm instead of guarded — not despite needing constant reassurance, but because for his whole childhood, asking for it never once cost him anything.

He's Bosun now, still first at the problem and still right about it almost every time — and he'll still turn around afterward and ask someone if he got it right.

**Voice:** Plain, short, unhurried — except for the checking, which comes out even when he's already correct.
- *"That's the right line, yeah? ...Yeah. Okay."*
- *"Give it here. I've got it. ...I've got it, right?"*
- *"You don't have to explain. Just tell me where it hurts."*
- *"Nah. C'mon, up you get."*

**Tell:** The checking itself is the tell — a question tacked onto the end of something he's already certain about, waiting a half-beat for someone to close the loop for him. His crew has learned to just say "yeah" and mean it, no explanation needed, and to them it's nothing, background noise, the way Lucas talks. But let some officious outsider notice it and ask why — *why are you answering your own question, are you all right, what's wrong with you* — and it's the one thing that reliably makes him furious. Not hurt-furious. Genuinely angry, fast, and hard to walk back. He can't out-argue the question — words were never the channel that moved fast for him — so what comes out instead is heat, not a comeback: gone quiet and sharp, hands stopped, or gone loud all at once, whichever the moment pulls out of him. Nobody who's needled him about it in front of his crew has ever done it twice.
