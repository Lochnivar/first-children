# Logan Moreau, Duke of Ryeleigh

**World:** Iolanu | **Gender:** Male | **Race:** Human  
**Status:** Sketch  
**Role:** Political operator — governs Ryeleigh through relentless, granular competence: logistics, treaties, infrastructure, succession planning. Also a formidable swordsman, rider, and tactician — the same wiring that made him fluent in statecraft made him fluent in anything masterable through repetition and attention rather than emotional performance.

**Appearance:** *(open)*

**Backstory:** His mother died bringing him into the world. His father, Duke Aldric Moreau, went under with a grief he never fully surfaced from, and for the better part of two years the infant heir was handed through a rotation of wet-nurses, nursery staff, and stewards — competently fed, competently kept, functionally unwitnessed. No single person was cruel to him; the cruelty, if it was one, was turnover — nobody stayed long enough to become a face he could learn. By the time his father resurfaced enough to really look at his son, something had already set.

The one person who stayed — a nurse named Perrine, who outlasted three of her own replacements by simply refusing reassignment — never tried to talk it out of him. She showed up at the same hour every day and did the same competent things in the same order, for years, without asking him to perform anything back. It's the only language that ever landed. He didn't learn "I love you." He learned "the same person, reliably, doing the necessary thing." When he became duke, he ran Ryeleigh the way Perrine ran a nursery, and it's the only fluent thing about him — except it turned out not to be the *only* one. The same wiring made him fluent in anything else that could be drilled instead of spoken: swordwork, tactics, riding, ledgers, languages. If it could be mastered through repetition rather than performed emotionally, he was already good at it before anyone noticed he was trying. People assume the brilliance and the distance are the same thing — cold because cerebral. They aren't related. He'd trade the sword arm for the one instinct he doesn't have, if trading were how it worked.

He is not broken and doesn't read as needing fixed. People who love him don't go looking for a hidden emotive Logan underneath — that Logan isn't in there waiting. They learn to read the granary and the bridge, the same way Perrine, without ever naming it, taught him that showing up counts as saying it.

There's exactly one place the gap doesn't hold: children too young to expect anything performed back. Something in him that never learned to answer a courtier's raised eyebrow answers a baby's grasping fist immediately, unthinkingly — the same way Perrine's hands once answered his. Put an infant or a small child in front of him and his face does the thing it does for no one else: lights up, unguarded, entirely readable, gone the instant he notices anyone's watching. Nobody who's seen it once forgets it, because it's the only time the rumor of a cold, calculating duke fails on contact with the actual man. He's never been able to explain why it's different with the littles. It just is. Maybe because nothing about a baby requires him to get the performance right, and it turns out that was always the only thing standing in the way.

**Voice:** Measured, precise, faintly formal even in private. Talks about feelings by talking about logistics.
- *"The eastern granary is at capacity. I'd rather not need it."*
- *"I don't require an apology. I require the bridge repaired before the thaw."*
- *"You'll have to tell me what you need from me. I won't guess correctly."*
- *"I'm told I'm difficult to read. I've never known what to do about that."*
- *(to a child, when he thinks no one's listening) "There you are. Yes. Hello."*

**Tell:** Under real emotional weight, he doesn't do anything — that's the tell. Mid-conversation, whatever small incidental motion he's making (a ring turned once around his finger, a pen tapped) stops dead, and his eyes hold one point half a second too long before he resumes at the exact same measured pace, as if nothing landed. People close to him have learned that the stillness *is* the reaction. Everyone else just thinks he's unbothered — and because the stillness never cracks under pressure, the court has decided it's a deliberate weapon, a poker face wielded on purpose. He has never corrected this. Partly he doesn't know how; partly the misread is politically useful and he's not above letting it work for him even though it isn't real.

**Relationships:** Paired with a not-yet-sketched Duchess — a "butterfly," socially fluent where he isn't, who serves as his interface to the world without ever speaking for him. She doesn't manage him or compensate for him; she's fluent in a different language and chose to learn his rather than requiring him to learn hers. Open hook for a future character sketch.
