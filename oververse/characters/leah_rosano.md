# Leah Rosano

**World:** Iolanu | **Gender:** Female | **Race:** Human  
**Status:** Sketch  
**Role:** Soprano. Known company-wide for two things that turn out to be the same thing: she's the one who gets sent to fix backstage morale — a diva mid-tantrum, a stagehand who just got bad news — and always does it with a joke instead of a lecture. And she's the one who keeps insisting on singing a signature aria that sits just above her actual range, not for acclaim, but because of what it feels like to reach for something almost too big for her.

**Backstory:** Came into the company (and the family that raised her) early and easily, nothing dramatic to explain who she turned out to be — she just arrived, and was loved, and grew up light. No wound required. She started singing before she had the range for anything, and by the time she had real training, the aria everyone privately agrees is above her had already become *hers* — the one she won't retire for something easier.

People peg her fast: flighty, giggly, sweet, not serious. She lets them. It's not an act — she is exactly that warm, that easily delighted — but she's noticed, long ago, precisely what her own laugh does to a room that's braced for a fight or gone silent with grief, and she has never once mistaken that effect for an accident. A performed version of "harmless and delightful" wouldn't get through anyone's guard. Hers does, because it's real, and because she's decided — deliberately, every time — to spend it on the person who needs the crack in their armor most that day.

Her actual instrument isn't the range she keeps chasing. Underneath the reach, she's a genuine high alto — rich, rare — and on the occasions she drops the aria and just sings from where her voice actually lives, the effect is close to magic. It's the kind of voice that could sing a Sarah Brightman to tears. She knows exactly how good it is. She still goes for the other note almost every time anyway, because the aria is the fun one — the thrill of reaching for something bigger than she technically has. Her real voice was never something she had to earn or prove. It's just there, whenever she actually wants it.

The alto is where she lives. The aria is where she dreams.

**Voice:** Warm, quick, teasing without an edge to it. Laughs easily and often — including at herself.
- *"I'm going to miss that note again tonight. Come anyway."*
- *"You don't have to tell me what's wrong. Just don't leave yet."*
- *"That was terrible. Do it again, but worse — I want to see you commit."*
- *"I didn't say I was good at it. I said I love it."*

**Tell:** When she cracks the top note — which is often, since the piece asks more than she has — she doesn't flinch, apologize, or push through gritted teeth. She laughs, mid-note, and finishes the phrase laughing. Audiences have come to love that as much as the nights she lands it clean, because it's the same thing either way: her, reaching, delighted regardless of whether she gets there.
