# Skyfall

**Status:** Proposed World - In Development
**Framework Conformance:** Active - See framework summary in [../skyfall.md](../skyfall.md)
**Last Updated:** 2026-07-28

## Overview

Skyfall is a world the **Greater Dragons** built for themselves and their Lesser Dragon children — later crashed by **the Four Pantheons**, a cohort of 54 First Children who seeded their repository species there as worshippers. Those species' competing mage factions, built in the image of their divine patrons' suit rivalries, raised magical **dungeons** that spawned monsters past any ability to contain them and overran the ground. Survivors now live in floating magi-tech cities, traveling by airship, raiding the uninhabitable surface for resources. Only the skies are safe.

Beneath the mortal survival story runs a dragon-tier one: the Greater Dragons went silent rather than fight a war that would destroy the world either way, and struck an unexplained bargain with the Pantheons to stay out entirely. Their Lesser Dragon children don't know why, and that unanswered silence has split them into two warring factions — one holding faith, one calling it betrayal.

**Core Theme:** What's owed to the abandoned, and whether restraint from the powerful reads as wisdom or complicity from below.

## The Catastrophe (Origin of the Now)

- **Cause:** Competing mage factions / schools / orders (FC-repository species) created their own magical dungeons — rival traditions built in the image of their divine patrons among the Four Pantheons, whose own suit rivalries the mortal factions inherited and amplified.
- **Trigger — RESOLVED, cascade via inherited genre one-upmanship:** No sabotage. The Four Pantheons' suit rivalries are ordinary gamer-culture pettiness — Hard Mode players sneering at Social Sim players, Sandbox purists at odds with Systems' clockwork-minded rivals — genre-snobbery that doesn't require developed selfhood, so even personality-light, nearly-blank First Children have it for free, no crystallization needed. Rival mage-schools inherited that contempt wholesale and tried to out-build each other's dungeons to win the argument (mine is harder, mine doesn't need luck, mine out-produces yours) — not to end the world. The decisive failure was **critical mass**: the number of competing dungeons exceeded any ability to contain them, and spawned monsters swamped every containment effort. Nobody sabotaged anything; everybody was showing off, and the showing off didn't stop scaling on its own.
- **Why nobody just destroyed the dungeons at the source:** Overwhelmed, not incompetent — too many, too fast. Containment was swamped, not skipped.
- **Status of the apocalypse now:** **Settled grim equilibrium**, not an ongoing worsening spike. People have built lives around the sky-only reality.

## The Four Pantheons (The 54)

- **What they are:** A cohort of 54 First Children — by no means an exhaustive slice of Hypatia's FC population, just the ones who organized into this particular game — structured like a deck of cards: **four suits of thirteen, plus two wild cards.**
- **Why these 54 chose this hobby:** Unlike Hegemon/Stratara's Strategists (personality-heavy FC pursuing contested, agenda-driven games over a board), the Four Pantheons arrived **naive** — ancient in knowledge, but nearly blank in personality. A game built around discovering who you become through worship only appeals to FC with nothing of their own to bring to a contested board. This isn't a game about winning; it's a game about becoming.
- **Suits are genre, not theology.** Each suit encodes a genre of fun at the FC level — what kind of game its members find satisfying to run. This is never mortal-legible; Skyfall's worshippers only ever see the fractured, individuated output (see The Grid, below), never the organizing logic. Apparent incoherence within a suit (why do the god of War and the god of Winter belong together?) is intentional — evidence of a real organizing principle mortals have no access to, not proof there isn't one.
  - **Spades — Hard Mode.** Conflict, adversity, consequence.
  - **Clubs — Sandbox Mode.** Growth, spread, multiplication with minimal interference.
  - **Diamonds — Systems Mode.** Intricate mechanisms — economies, law, craft — and watching mortals optimize or break inside them.
  - **Hearts — Social Sim Mode.** Mortal relationships as the actual game: romance, court intrigue, rivalry, betrayal.
  - **The two Wild Cards** refuse to commit to a genre — the most unpredictable members of the cohort, and the hardest for any mortal cult to build a coherent theology around.
- **"We create our gods," literalized.** FC communicate through **waveform interference** (see [First Children](../../cosmology/first-children.md)). Sustained, focused worship — millions of minds converging expectation onto the same pattern, for centuries — is interference at exactly the layer FC operate on. A naive First Child doesn't just acquire a reputation from worship; it acquires an actual personality. The gods are completely real, ancient, and powerful — what mortals invented wasn't their existence, it was their selves.
- **Rank = Crystallization, not seniority.** A god's rank within its suit tracks how much selfhood it has accumulated from worship, not raw seniority or innate power. The Ace of a suit is the oldest, most devotedly worshipped, most fully "written" god in that domain. Low cards are newer or thinner cults whose patron is still mostly blank. Crystallization is **not fixed** — rank can theoretically rise or slip as worship shifts, making this a live mechanic rather than static lore.
- **The Free Anchor — why boredom didn't make them leave.** Per the general law that First Children need an ongoing stake or attention drifts to the next hobby (see [First Children](../../cosmology/first-children.md#investment-and-attention-general-law) — Malachor's autopilot Church is the demonstrating counter-case), boredom alone predicts the Pantheons should be long gone. They aren't, because worship turned out to do something none of them expected: Quantumancy's Persistence Problem normally requires an Anchor — an artifact, or a First Child's own sustained attention — to hold a collapsed state against drift (see [Quantum Foundation](../../metaphysics/quantum-foundation.md), [QSON](../../metaphysics/qson.md)). Sustained mass worship *functions as* an Anchor — distributed, self-sustaining, and costing the worshipped First Child nothing. None of the 54 arrived understanding this; they found it by leaving the hobby running long enough. Came for the hobby, stayed for the experiment.
- **Playing god as variable-tuning, not customer service.** Any *direct* Quantumancy the Pantheons spent shaping Skyfall would cost them the very attention the free Anchor lets them stop spending — so the one lever left that doesn't undo the discovery is which prayers get answered, which miracles land, which cults get quietly encouraged. That's why the performance of godhood never got old: it's the only knob still available. Keep this **fiddling, not methodology** — the 54 are prodding something they don't understand, not running controlled experiments with hypotheses. That distinction is what keeps them naive rather than sliding into Hegemon/Stratara-style calculating Strategists. **A sufficiently sustained, deliberate belief campaign could reshape a god on purpose** — not a reserved future weapon so much as the thing the 54 are already doing to each other and themselves, constantly, without fully grasping it.
- **Mortals as unwitting co-authors.** The Pantheons' territorial claim over the Lesser Dragons ("ours now, get over it") doesn't have to be innate divine cruelty. It's plausibly the first thing early, frightened, self-interested worshippers ever wanted from their new gods — protection, dominance, a claim staked against a rival — and the naive FC became that, because nobody had given them anything else to be yet. Skyfall's mortals aren't only victims of the Pantheons' arrival; they are, unknowingly, part of what made their gods what they are.
- **What they did to Skyfall:** Crashed a world that wasn't theirs — built by the Greater Dragons for themselves and their Lesser Dragon children — and seeded it with FC-repository species as worshippers. In the first millennium or two after arrival, the Four Pantheons squatted, spread across the land, and built nations while the newly-arrived repository species organized their own competing magical traditions in the image of their divine patrons' suit rivalries. Those traditions became the schools/factions/orders that built the dungeons (see The Catastrophe, above). The pantheon didn't intend the apocalypse that followed — the competitive spread of their own worship did that on its own.
- **Openly worshipped:** Mortals on Skyfall worship the Four Pantheons as legitimate religion — temples, myths, and iconography genuinely reflect the four suits and the rank structure within them. This is not a secret the dragons alone are in on.
- **The dragons call them crashers.** Mortals call them gods. Both are right.

### The Grid (Full Reimagining — supersedes the original per-suit domain lists)

The original design gave each suit its own internal list of 13 domains, generated top-down from genre-of-fun. That produced a Hard Mode suit that read as a war-and-death monoculture and several domains (Guilds, Measurement, Hoarding, Executioners) that felt like RPG-splatbook categories rather than something a real worshipper would organize a religion around.

**Replaced with a grid:** 13 shared domains grounded in real comparative mythology (see [comparative-mythology-worksheet.md](comparative-mythology-worksheet.md) for the diagnostic pass that produced it), each given four takes — one per suit's genre. Ace is Seasons, King is War/Glory/Honor/Freedom, Jack is the classical elements, down to 2, each suit's own Terminal State pushed to its worst final form. A handful of domains span multiple houses on their own terms rather than fitting the grid (Death touches Spades/Diamonds/Hearts/Clubs each differently; the Sun is exempted from the Pantheons entirely, claimed instead by the Dragon layer — see below).

**Depth no longer tracks rank.** The old crystallization rule (Ace–Jack fully written, number cards thinning toward blankness) only worked when rank was a stand-in for a cult's age and popularity. Now that rank is just a domain axis, every card gets full treatment regardless of position — see [four-pantheons-roster.md](four-pantheons-roster.md) for the full grid and [pantheon-sketches.md](pantheon-sketches.md) for the complete worship-sketch of all 52.

**Cold glosses, warm worship:** unchanged — individual god descriptions read clinical even for warm-sounding portfolios (Love, Glory, Diplomacy). That's the FC's-eye-view of their own mechanic, not a tonal accident. What worshippers experience is the crystallized, worship-shaped surface; the cold gloss is what's underneath it.

## The Now (Present-Day Status Quo)

- Survivors live **primarily** on floating cities ("primarily" leaves room for other enclaves — see POV note).
- Travel by **magi-tech airships**.
- The ground is uninhabitable due to monster hordes. **Raids** descend for resources; **settlement attempts are overrun and destroyed.**
- **Why raids work where settlements don't:** Monsters are **slow to mobilize, not weak.** Raids are in-grab-out before the monsters can rally and respond. Even so, raiding is genuinely risky.

### Raiding as a Structure
- Not purely opportunistic. Raids range from **window-based** (wait for a relatively safe period, grab what's available) to **goal-based** (we need this specific thing now, which changes/ignores the "safe" timing).
- Goal-based raids shift the risk calculus — they accept danger or manufacture temporary safety. (Exact mechanism — higher casualties vs. force-projection/perimeter-clearing — **not yet decided**; this could map onto faction wealth/capability differences between cities.)

### Tech Level
- Goal: **as low-tech as possible.** Likely resolution: the floating cities and core float-mechanism are **inherited dragon-grade infrastructure** the survivors barely maintain rather than invented — the one "high" concession sits in the origin, while the mortals are scavenger-maintainers. (**Not yet confirmed** — alternative is survivors engineered the cities themselves post-collapse. See Open Questions.)

## Point of View / Scope Discipline

- **The sky-dwellers only know about the sky-dwellers.** Limited POV is deliberate.
- Other survivor enclaves (ground bunkers, nomads, stranger things) are intentionally left unresolved — to be revealed during storytelling as a twist, not explained up front. Consistent with "raid fast and leave" (raiders have no reason to encounter anyone else).

## The Dragon Layer

### Greater Dragons
- **Built this world** for themselves and their Lesser Dragon children.
- **Not involved in the present.** Offstage (consistent with the universe's general creator-goes-offstage pattern). Per genus.md they cannot be permanently destroyed — absence now does not require death, just departure.
- **Why they didn't fight:** The Greater Dragons concluded that any direct conflict with the Four Pantheons would destroy whatever world it happened on. Per genus.md, Greater Dragons are functionally undefeatable and sibling-equals to FC, not subordinates — this isn't a story about weakness, it's a story about a fight nobody could actually win.
- **Why they didn't evacuate the Lesser Dragons either:** **The Skyfall Bargain.** The Four Pantheons offered the Greater Dragons something else — off-screen, unspecified, deliberately reserved for future development as the other side of the Bargain — in exchange for staying out of Skyfall entirely. The Greater Dragons took it. Evacuating the Lesser Dragons afterward would have looked exactly like reneging on that deal, or repositioning for a war they'd already decided they couldn't win.
- **Deliberately unresolved — from the Lesser Dragons' POV:** None of the above is known to the Lesser Dragons. To them, their parents simply went silent and never came, and never explained why. That silence — not a settled answer — is the wound. Keep this a genuine, unanswered mystery in-story; the ambiguity is the point (angst fuel, per POV Scope Discipline above).

### The Sun-King Myth
- **Aurelion the Firstborn** (see [Genus Draconis](../../races/draconis/genus.md) — gold, wise, eldest of the Greater Dragons) is believed to have withdrawn into the sun itself after the Skyfall Bargain: visible every day, presiding, unreachable. The stars are held to be his court; any moons Skyfall develops are his courtesans. No roster of named attendant stars is being committed to — left open for narrative use as needed, not pre-populated.
- **Started as Eastern doctrine, now universal.** The belief began among Eastern Lesser Dragons specifically — Aurelion is Eastern's own lineage-parent (gold/metallic, wisdom-coded, matching Eastern's serpentine, patient temperament) — but has since spread past dragon culture entirely into mortal folklore across Skyfall. It is not a rival cult to the Four Pantheons' organized religion: no temples, no answered prayers, no priesthood. It functions as shared cultural legend, not competing worship.
- **Same shared fact, opposite readings.** Because the belief is now universal, it's no longer a point of disagreement between Eastern and Western — the disagreement moves to what the fact *means*. Eastern reads Aurelion's visible, daily presence as evidence of patient, watching wisdom: proof restraint is the right posture, since their own father models exactly that restraint from above. Western reads the identical fact as the cruelest possible version of the abandonment — not an absent, unreachable father, but one who is *provably right there, every single day*, and still hasn't come down. Visible-but-unreachable sharpens the existing East/West wound rather than resolving it.
- **Deliberately not narrator-confirmed.** Whether Aurelion is actually in the sun, actually watching, actually able to act — none of that is settled in-story. This stays exactly as unresolved as the rest of the Skyfall Bargain, above; the myth is a belief people hold, not a fact the audience is handed.
- **Resolves the Sky & Storm gap from the pantheon side without adding a suit card** — the sun isn't claimed by the Four Pantheons at all. Storm remains the only new Weather-cluster addition under consideration, proposed for Diamonds (see [comparative-mythology-worksheet.md](comparative-mythology-worksheet.md)).

### Lesser Dragons
- **Still present but population-limited.** Ground down by **three-front attrition**: monsters, human/FC-repository species, and **each other**.
- This explains "few left" without a separate cause.

### East/West Dichotomy + Infighting
- **Eastern vs. Western dragon** cultural/morphological split among Lesser Dragons, with active fighting between the factions. This is the concrete answer to "ground down by three-front attrition... and **each other**" above — the third front is this war.
- Grounded in existing canon: lineage is already determined by Greater Dragon parent, and color already carries lineage meaning (chromatic = aggressive, metallic = cooperative). East/West is **lineage-deep**: different Greater Dragon parents built their children on different templates —
  - **Western:** winged, territorial, fire, "burn it all / the monsters can be fought." Lineage-parent: **Vel'kazar the Eternal Flame** (red, fierce, aggressive).
  - **Eastern:** serpentine, weather/sky-tied, wisdom-coded, "the plague is weather, you don't fight it, you endure/redirect it." Lineage-parent: **Aurelion the Firstborn** (gold, wise, eldest) — see The Sun-King Myth, above.
- **Why they still fight despite shared extinction:** The temperament split (already lineage-deep, pre-catastrophe) was activated into open war by the arrival of the Four Pantheons and the silence of the Skyfall Bargain that followed. Two postures hardened around the same silence:
  - **Eastern = faith + restraint.** Trusts that the Greater Dragons' non-intervention is wisdom, not abandonment — and grounds that trust in cold self-preservation as much as hope: without the Greater Dragons standing behind them, provoking the Four Pantheons risks the Pantheons simply erasing what's left of Lesser Dragon-kind. Eastern actively restrains Western from striking at the monsters *or* the Pantheons' mortal worshippers, on the theory that either target risks the same catastrophic response.
  - **Western = disillusionment + aggression.** Counseled patience for the first millennium or two after the Pantheons arrived and began squatting — "don't overreact" — and has since concluded that patience only produced more deaths, not rescue. Doesn't cleanly separate "the monsters" from "the gods who caused them"; wants unrestrained war against both the horde and the Pantheons' mortal cult infrastructure (temples, worshippers). Views Eastern's restraint not as caution but as active complicity: every Lesser Dragon death since is, in Western's eyes, a death Eastern's restraint policy caused.
  - This is why shared extinction doesn't suppress the infighting: it isn't a disagreement about tactics, it's a live accusation of ongoing responsibility for the dying. Neither side can back down without conceding they let their kin die for nothing.
- **Leaning (not fully closed):** the split is **both** pre-catastrophe lineage *and* catastrophe-activated — the temperament was always latent in the two lineages, but the Pantheons' arrival and the Bargain's silence is what turned it into an active war rather than a background cultural difference.

## Continuity / Framework Hooks

- **FC infiltration angle:** This fits the established pattern where FC create/seed something to prove a point or expand reach without fully reckoning with consequences (cf. Dracari, the Greater-Dragon/FC taxonomic grudge). Here, the Four Pantheons (54 FC) seeded repo species into a world that wasn't theirs, and those species broke it.
- **Skyfall as outlier, not routine:** The Four Pantheons' open claim over the Lesser Dragons pushes right up against genus.md's default FC/Greater-Dragon relationship ("tolerate, don't confront... respect mandatory or face eternal war"). The Greater Dragons absorbing this via the Skyfall Bargain rather than escalating means this world sits near the edge of what that sibling non-aggression pact can survive — a live fuse for later, not a settled status quo.
- **Grievance rhymes:** Greater Dragons opposing/resenting FC is already canon (genus.md: "Willing to oppose FC"). A dragon-built world wrecked by an FC-species sideshow is a natural extension of that sibling tension.
- **Distinct from Delverium:** Delverium is the gamified LitRPG dungeon world (Zorathar's meritocracy experiment). **This world is explicitly NOT LitRPG** — its dungeons are a catastrophic, accidental industrial-scale disaster, not a game. Keep the contrast sharp so the two "dungeon worlds" don't blur.
