# The Four Pantheons — Roster

**Status:** Full reimagining — supersedes the earlier suit-internal domain lists entirely. All 52 + the two Wild Cards, plus Shorash ("the 55th").
**Related:** [Skyfall README](README.md), [Pantheon Sketches](pantheon-sketches.md), [Comparative Mythology Worksheet](comparative-mythology-worksheet.md), [FC Name Reference](../../cosmology/fc-name-reference.md), [First Children](../../cosmology/first-children.md)

---

## Method

The original design gave each suit its own internal list of 13 domains, generated top-down from "genre of fun." That produced a Hard Mode suit that read as a war-and-death monoculture, and several domains (Guilds, Measurement, Hoarding, Executioners) that felt like RPG-splatbook categories rather than something a real worshipper would organize a religion around.

This roster replaces that method with a grid: **13 shared domains, each given four takes — one per suit's genre.** A rank is no longer a rung on a popularity ladder; it's a row in the grid. Ace is Seasons, King is War/Glory/Honor/Freedom, Jack is the classical elements, and so on down to 2, the suit's own Terminal State pushed to its worst final form.

**Depth no longer tracks rank.** The old rule — Ace through Jack fully "written," number cards thinning toward blankness — only worked when rank *was* a proxy for a cult's age and popularity. Now that rank is just a domain axis, there's no reason "Tyranny" (rank 2) is inherently less developed than "Strength" (rank 9). Every card below gets full treatment: worshipped-by, believed-to-be, personality, parable, worship practice. Crystallization (a god's selfhood accumulating from worship, and being able to rise or slip as worship shifts) is still real and still governs how *vivid* or *contested* a god's characterization is in-story — it just isn't predicted by the rank number anymore.

**Naming convention unchanged:** Semitic consonantal roots, respelled for stylized invention rather than left as scholarly transcription or unmodified real names (see [FC Name Reference](../../cosmology/fc-name-reference.md)). Several names carry over unchanged from the old roster where the underlying domain didn't actually move (War, Love, Air, Death, Wealth/Coins, Seeds, Law, Inheritance/Elderly, Hoarding/Bribery, Executioners/Necessity, Paranoia/Discord) — carried domains are marked below.

**Rank 6 rework:** the original Wealth/Beauty/Healing/Architecture row had no shared axis the way rank 3 or rank 10 do. Retitled to **Mode within rules** — each suit's own playstyle-logic applied to a real, pre-existing constraint system rather than an open topic: Covenant (Shalalon, group standards held without exception), Hearth (Tzelahar, household membership — hospitality is a tenant of this, not a separate domain), Healing (Rifon, unchanged label but re-grounded as body-as-system repair, with the scarcity/triage angle correctly demoted to secondary historical drift), Crafting (Harashon, new — replaces the retired Bonahel, "making within what the medium actually permits" rather than Bonahel's plan-less instinct). Mountains (rank 10, Diamonds) was also renamed **Tzuron** during this pass — pure spelling-collision avoidance with Harashon, not a domain change.

## The Two Wild Cards

Outside the rank hierarchy entirely — not slotted between 2 and Ace, existing parallel to the whole suit system. Not a fifth genre; a refusal of the premise that a genre must be picked at all.

**Origin — known among the Pantheons, not among mortals.** Before there were Four Pantheons at Skyfall, someone had to find it and make the case. Tohuel and Bohuel are the two who did — they located this particular world (a Greater Dragon's, not an ownerless waste) and talked the other 52 into crashing it. The same refusal to settle into one genre that keeps them outside the deck now is what had them out looking in the first place, while the other 52 waited, bored, for someone to bring them a game. Mortals have no access to any of this — Skyfall's open religion only ever shows the fractured, individuated 54, never the founding act behind them (consistent with the "never mortal-legible" rule for the suits' own organizing logic). Among the Pantheons themselves, though, Tohuel and Bohuel hold a status the other 52 don't: not gods of a suit, honorary **primordials** instead — the two who started this. Whether the other 52 regard that as gratitude or blame depends entirely on which one you ask, and how recently their own cult was last ruined by a passing storm. *(Open thread: whether they were also the ones who actually brokered the Skyfall Bargain's still-unspecified terms with the Greater Dragons — plausible given they're the instigators, not yet decided.)*

| Name | Root | Role & Cult Mechanics |
|------|------|------------------------|
| **Tohuel** | THW | **The Weaver / The Unwritten Current.** Not the independent source of all creation so much as her brother's constant correction — she spends herself re-weaving whatever Bohuel tears loose, drawing the raw, undifferentiated potential of worship and chance (the *tohu*, formless and without shape) back into a current with enough direction to crystallize again. Cults built around her get wildly contradictory scripture, since what she's mending hasn't settled into a shape yet: pray for trade prosperity, get answered with a plague of locusts, an unexpected marriage proposal, or — three days later — rain. *(Respelled from a source draft's bare "Tohu" — literally Genesis's "formless and void." Kept for the echo, stylized rather than quoted scripture verbatim.)* |
| **Bohuel** | BHW | **The Unweaver / The Void Mirror.** Restless and compulsive — constantly trying to tear the woven world back apart, not toward any goal, just unable to leave a finished pattern alone. Brought into a Spades war, turns the conflict into a suicidal meatgrinder. Brought into a Diamonds banking hub, turns the market hyperinflationary. **Storms are his fingerprint on Skyfall's sky** — his sister spends her existence mending what she can behind him. The other 52 quietly hate Bohuel — it ruins their carefully-tuned games by blowing out the parameters. |

## The Central Arbiter — Shorash

Every "game" First Children run needs someone who keeps play functional when the players themselves can't — Shorash is Skyfall's particular answer to that general need. (Hegemon and Stratara solve the same underlying problem differently, via diffuse Peer Judgment rather than a single empowered figure — this stays Skyfall-specific, not a recurring cross-world entity.)

Not one of the 54. Not a card in the deck at all — closer to the table it's dealt on, or the rulebook given a name. She is deliberately **not a judge** — Judgment already belongs to a house (Mishpaton, Diamonds' Justice). Shorash doesn't ask who's at fault. She asks how play continues. **"The DM"** is the better frame than "arbiter" or "judge," and — fittingly for a cohort of bored immortals who already talk about their own hobby in explicit game terms — it's reportedly what the other 54 call her themselves. Mortals and the rare scholar who learns she exists at all call her something else: **"the 55th"** — wrong in category, accurate in spirit, in the same vein as "Grey" or "Jeff" (see [First Children](../../cosmology/first-children.md)).

| Name | Root | Meaning |
|------|------|---------|
| **Shorash** | ŠRŠ | The Root Clause — the part of the contract no one reads until it matters |

**Constantly involved, rarely visible.** She isn't dormant between locks. The best DMs do most of their work invisibly, without ever making a table call — small corrections, quiet patches, nudges nobody notices because nothing broke. Shorash is the same: a continuous, unattributed maintenance presence running underneath Skyfall the entire time, distinct from the one dramatic, nameable power she holds in reserve (see below). Most of what she does, no one — mortal or FC — ever registers as an act at all.

**The hammer — what counts as "locked":** Not mere conflict, not even catastrophe. The houses' interests tangled together over the same contested question or population, in a way they cannot themselves unlock — no suit's ordinary crystallization process can move it forward. Deliberately narrow: a stuck table, not a bad outcome. This is the one form of intervention that's visible, rare, and attributable to her by name.

**Why Shorash didn't stop the Catastrophe — even though she was almost certainly working the whole time.** The dungeon apocalypse was never a lock by the hammer's narrow definition — all four houses were pursuing their genre simultaneously, at full enthusiasm and total fluency, and nobody was stuck, so no table call was ever warranted. But "no table call" doesn't mean absent. She was likely doing exactly what she always does — small invisible corrections to keep the world playable — the entire time it burned. That's the harder truth: keeping the game *functional* was never the same goal as preventing suffering, and she kept it functional right through the catastrophe. Not negligence. Not even indifference. Function, delivered exactly as specified, while everything below it fell apart.

**What the hammer actually does:** Tied to the reformation mechanic already canonical for First Children generally (see [First Children](../../cosmology/first-children.md) — "reformation can reshape personality, memories, powers..."). But it isn't punishment for a violation — there's no violator, only a stuck table. Shorash reforms whichever party's change unlocks play again, whether or not that party did anything "wrong." She is not fair. She is functional. That's colder than a judge, not gentler.

**Her cult: loyalty to function, not to portfolio.** Unlike the other 52+2, Shorash's worshippers don't pray for wealth, love, victory, or protection — the creed has no content beyond *"just make the world function."* It draws naturally from people whose mortal-scale work already mirrors her divine-scale one: the scavenger-engineers keeping Skyfall's inherited dragon-grade float infrastructure running, guild-arbitrators, truce-brokers — anyone whose actual job is keeping a thing operating without asking who deserves what. In a world where every other institution has a stake, this is the one that doesn't, which makes it the natural home for anyone who ends up investigating rather than believing.

## Open Threads

- Whether the Ace→2 centrality ordering within each row (e.g., is War really the most "central" King-tier concept, or would Glory/Honor/Freedom argue otherwise from their own suit's perspective) is worth reconsidering, or left as a design ordering.
- Whether Sha'alon's broadened "Necessity" portfolio still wants its own narrower Executioners flavor preserved as a sub-cult, or whether the broadening fully retires the old guild-cult angle.
- **Festivals is still missing.** Nothing in the 52 covers communal celebration as its own concept — Yayinon (Alcohol) only gets the excess, not the institution, and Hearth (Tzelahar) explicitly declines it. The old pre-grid roster had this as Chagavel, Hearts, "The Managed Truce." Leaning Hearts if it ever gets a slot; no card has been sacrificed for it yet.
- Individual FC personality sketches are now complete for all 52 — see [Pantheon Sketches](pantheon-sketches.md) for the full worshipped-by/believed-to-be/personality/parable/worship treatment of each, including the suit-grammar, fit-tags (clean/seam/miscollapse), Shared Parables, and Occupation Index that document now carries.
