## What This Replaces

The archived [trial-mechanics.md §1](archive/trial-mechanics-era/trial-mechanics.md) opens: "The FC activated the QWAN on Earth. That is the complete origin of everything that follows." That account is **wrong about the cause and right about almost everything downstream.** The Summer of Chaos, the premature detonations, the pineal activations, the walls, the Commission, the KPI narrative — all of that still happens, on the same rough timeline, for the same felt reasons. What changes is *why* the field flipped when it did, and what that implies about Earth's entire prior history.

Earth is not a world an FC flipped a switch on in anyone's living memory. Earth is a world an FC switched a field *off* on, forty thousand years ago, and forgot to check on again until the switch finally gave out.

## Act I — The Experiment

Every Hypatia world is somebody's question. Bahvayin asks whether diverse species integrate or destroy each other under pressure. Rakaiah asks whether QWAN suppresses technological development. Delverium asks how far mortals swim in the quantum sea. Earth's creator — the same FC who, decades into the Trial, will later force the Reclamation — asked a different question, and asked it the way FC ask everything: carelessly, without a review board, without an exit plan.

**The question: what does a civilization build if it develops at the wrong field state, and what happens to it when the field is finally corrected?**

Like every world he could have touched, Earth's native, intended field state is HiRB — the same asserted baseline every other Hypatia world runs on. There was never anything structurally special about Earth that required it to sit at the universal default LoRB. What happened instead is that shortly after asserting it — during the same documented visitation window that human mythology remembers as Gray contact and sample collection, roughly forty thousand years ago (see [archived trial-mechanics.md §8.2](archive/trial-mechanics-era/trial-mechanics.md)) — he built something to hold the field down below where he'd just put it, and walked away to see what would happen.

That something is **the suppressor.** Functionally, it is a Nullwraith's talent running at civilizational scale with nobody attending it: a standing mechanism that continuously resolves ambient superposition faster than the field restores it, denying both the biological interfaces (pineal, amygdala) and the planet's own chemistry the unresolved possibility space either would need to behave like a HiRB world. It does not generate the suppression from nothing. It burns through it — consuming the ambient reserve of unresolved quantum states (informally, the planet's **mana**) as fuel, the same resource any sustained magical practice draws on, just drawn down continuously, automatically, at planetary scale, for forty thousand years.

Nobody designed this as weapons denial, trial infrastructure, or mercy. It was a test. He did not come back to check the results for most of it. That is the entire explanation, and it is sufficient, because that is how every First Child in this framework behaves.

## The Physics: A Shelf Above the Floor

Resolution Bias is usually described as a world's field sitting at one of two discrete states, each stable without maintenance — a true binary, no gradient, no decay. Earth's suppressed state is not that. It is closer to a metastable **false vacuum**: a shelf held *above* the floor the field actually wants to occupy, propped there by something that requires active throughput to maintain, not by a clean FC-level assertion that simply holds "the way a valley remains a valley."

- **The floor is HiRB.** Lower, in the sense that matters here: more resolved, more decided, the state every quantum system in an unsuppressed Hypatia world eventually settles into. This is Earth's true calibration and always was.
- **The shelf is the suppressed state** — LoRB in every practical, felt sense, for every year the suppressor runs. But it isn't the universal default-LoRB every untouched world starts at. It's propped up, artificially, consuming fuel to stay there. Nothing about it is self-sustaining. It persists only because something is actively spending resource to keep it from collapsing to the floor it was always sitting above.

This distinction is why the suppressor's failure doesn't behave like a clean field-state flip. A true HiRB/LoRB assertion doesn't degrade. A prop that's been drawing down its own fuel supply for forty millennia does.

## Two Variables, Not One

Everything downstream falls out of treating **resolution readiness** and **mana density** as separate quantities that fail on separate timelines.

**Resolution readiness** is binary and structural — whether the field is currently being held above the floor or not. The moment the suppressor's throughput stops, there is nothing left propping the shelf up. The floor reasserts essentially immediately, because nothing was ever holding *it* in place; it was the natural result the suppressor had been fighting against the whole time. This is not a bubble that needs to nucleate and propagate — the suppressor's reach was already planetary and continuous while it ran, so its absence is felt everywhere at once, the moment it stops.

**Mana density** is a resource, not a state — the local reserve of unresolved superposition, the same stuff any practitioner's sustained use draws down and the field slowly restores (see [quantum-unified-theory.md](../../mechanics/quantum-unified-theory.md), "The Death of Magic: A Resolution Bias Restatement"). Forty thousand years of continuous suppression did not just hold the field down; it drew Earth's ambient reserve down with it, the way an aquifer is drawn down by decades of continuous pumping. Resolution readiness snaps back the instant the pump stops. The aquifer does not refill that fast. It refills by ordinary diffusion, from the rest of an HiRB multiverse the suppressor had been locally fighting, on a timescale of years, not seconds.

This is the whole mechanism. Everything else below is what falls out of it.

## Act II — The Shelf Drops

The suppressor runs dry. Not switched off by decision — exhausted, the way any continuously-operated draw against a finite reserve eventually is. There is no reason to assume anyone, FC or otherwise, chose the moment. It simply reached the end of what forty thousand years of throughput could sustain.

Resolution readiness reasserts across the planet at once. This is the event the archived material calls the Summer of Chaos, and nothing about its surface description needs to change:

- Explosive compounds are the shallowest-barrier systems on the planet — small, self-contained metastable pockets of their own, already weighted toward reaction, needing the least additional resolution pressure to tip. They go first and they go immediately, because resolution readiness is the one variable that doesn't wait on mana density at all. Military stockpiles detonate. Fuel becomes unstable. Engines run hot and fail.
- Pineal glands, which had hardware with nothing coherent to couple to for forty thousand years, suddenly have signal. Eight billion people experience activation simultaneously — not because an FC pushed a notification, but because the floor they were always going to sit on, the moment nothing held them off it, is finally under their feet.
- The walls, the Commission, the KPI narrative, the Gatelock economy — everything in [archived trial-mechanics.md §3 onward](archive/trial-mechanics-era/trial-mechanics.md) still happens, built by the same Homo genus staffers reacting to the same visible chaos, for the same institutional reasons. They are managing the end of an experiment nobody told them was running, not the start of one an FC just authorized. The Commission does not know the difference. It would not change what they do if they did.

What does not happen: an FC in a boardroom somewhere deciding to flip a switch. Nobody decided this. That's worse, in exactly the way the framework's tone already insists on — nobody is to blame in a satisfying way, there's no decision to be angry at, just an abandoned mechanism that ran until it couldn't.

## The Equalization Period

Mana density does not snap back. It diffuses back, the slow way, over roughly the ten years between Trial onset and [the Reclamation](../reclamation.md). This window is not simply "how long it took the Commission to stabilize" — it is physically load-bearing, and the Commission's stabilization is a symptom of it, not its cause.

Iolanu, across the whole of its existence, has been sitting at full HiRB saturation — mana-rich by comparison, the way any world that was never artificially drawn down would be. Earth, the moment the shelf drops, is resolution-ready but mana-starved: the field state matches Iolanu's, but the *reserve* behind it is still close to empty after forty thousand years of continuous draw-down.

Forcing a merge between two worlds at matched resolution readiness but wildly mismatched mana density is not a messy, uncurated absorption — it is simply destructive, the way connecting two reservoirs at matched elevation but wildly different pressure behind the gate produces a blowout rather than a flow. The ten years between Trial onset and the Reclamation is the time it takes Earth's reserve to rise far enough, by ordinary diffusion from the rest of the HiRB-saturated multiverse, that when the auto-merge actually lands, the two sides are close enough in mana density for the result to be a mechanically ugly, uncurated merge commit — exactly the tone [reclamation.md](../reclamation.md) already establishes — rather than an extinction event for one or both worlds.

**This is authorial fact, not a tracked countdown.** No character, no institution, no Tinkari sensing the field, knows this is why the Commission's decade feels fragile instead of settled, or why the FC's unrelated errand happens to land when it does instead of five years earlier or fifty years later. The timing is real. Nobody in the story gets to know why it's real. That's consistent with everything else this framework already says about how First Children operate: they don't maintain, they don't explain, and the one thing that would make this legible to the people living through it is exactly the thing nobody bothered to tell them.

## Act III — The Reclamation Finishes It

By the time the FC returns to repossess Iolanu, the equalization has gone far enough that the forced auto-merge is survivable chaos rather than mutual annihilation — which is the only reason [reclamation.md](../reclamation.md)'s whole account of the event (rifts as collateral damage, Homo Quantus merging clean, geography resolved by coordinate occupancy) gets to happen at all instead of ending both Books on page one.

The suppressor itself — long dormant, physically still present wherever it was built, never repaired, never revisited — does not survive the merge. It has no Iolanu equivalent, so by the merge's own "no Earth equivalent = clean apply, Earth equivalent exists = Earth wins" logic it should pass through untouched — but it is exactly the kind of structure the merge algorithm doesn't evaluate for importance before it starts tearing substrate apart to staple two worlds together. It goes the way the rifts do: not a deliberate transplant, not a deliberate destruction, just collateral damage from forcing two unrelated substrates into the same space. The FC doing the merging has no idea it's there, no idea it was ever his, and no reason to notice it's gone. He finishes an experiment he started forty thousand years earlier without knowing he started it or finished it.

That's the whole irony this is building toward, and it's worth stating plainly: the same carelessness that made him abandon the suppressor in the first place is the carelessness that destroys it for good, decades after a cleanup crew he never met built an entire civilization's survival strategy around a crisis he still doesn't know he caused.

## Side Effect: Patchy Returns

Mana diffusing back unevenly over the equalization period has one visible, in-world consequence worth keeping separate from the main mechanism: it is the missing trigger condition for [qwan.md](../../mechanics/qwan.md)'s "Nala's Accidental Push." That document already establishes that Earth's population (like every world that received Nala's mis-scoped commit) carries dormant Homo Quantus genetic potential — present, silent, needing "specific developmental conditions, environmental triggers" nobody had defined.

Rising local mana density during the equalization period is that trigger, and because the return is diffuse and uneven rather than global and simultaneous, talent expression during this window is patchy by the same logic: a latent carrier needs the local reserve to cross a threshold that is still, in most places, well below where it needs to be. Where it happens to cross early — for reasons that can stay geographically arbitrary, or can later be tied to something specific (proximity to wherever the suppressor sat; that's undecided and doesn't need resolving now) — someone with no context, no training, and no cultural framework for what's happening to them starts perceiving or doing something their culture has no name for. It is isolated, deniable, and easy for CORP or the Commission to misfile, right up until there's enough of a pattern for someone to notice one.

This is authorial-fact-level the same way the equalization timing is. Nobody connects early talent expression to a ten-year countdown nobody knows exists.
