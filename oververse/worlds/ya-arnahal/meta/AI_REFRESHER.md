# Ya'arnahal — AI Refresher

**Purpose:** Quick-reference for AI context when working on Ya'arnahal stories or world-building.
**Scope:** Premise, the System mechanics, geography, settler tracks, tone
**Canonical source:** [../../ya-arnahal.md](../../ya-arnahal.md)

---

## One-Line Summary

**A cold-rush frontier world opened by the Land Grants Authority (LGA) for a Hypatia-wide land run — no treasure resource, the land grant itself (bundled with a QWST homesteader package) is the draw, while the Authority's real motive is mesh density, distributed prospecting, and preemptive multi-race optics.** Tonal target: *Little House on the Prairie* domestic competence fused with *Call of the Wild* / *White Fang* isolation. Deliberately not a dungeon-crawl.

## The Land Grants Authority

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Not FC-run** | Staff/administrative body, chartered at Mercara's Cross — pattern closer to Earth's Commission than to an FC-owned world |
| **Likely backend** | Legara (contracts — land grants are contracts) / Nummus (ledger — claims as registered assets), inferred not confirmed |
| **Stated motive** | Generosity — free land for anyone willing to work it, open to all races at once |
| **Real motive (3-part)** | 1. Mesh density ("unworked land is under-meshed land") 2. Distributed prospecting (settlers find what surveys wouldn't, free) 3. Preemptive multi-race opening (avoids favoritism optics — echoes the Cross's own neutral-ground culture) |
| **No pusher race named** | Deliberately — naming one would defeat the preemptive-neutrality point |
| **Native-displacement angle** | Explicitly off the table by authorial choice — flora/fauna only, no pre-existing sapient population |

## The System (QWAN / QWST), Ya'arnahal-Specific

No new physics — this is a distribution-model variant of mechanics already canonical in [qwan.md](../../../mechanics/qwan.md).

| Layer | What it is | Ya'arnahal specifics |
|---|---|---|
| **Field (HiRB)** | Binary, world-level, already asserted | Not a Ya'arnahal design choice |
| **QWAN (pineal)** | Passive carrier, range-limited broadcast ("in range") | Same as everywhere — Ya'arnahal just has few enough settlers that the range-limit becomes visible instead of imperceptible |
| **QWST (amygdala)** | Reality-regulator / LitRPG UI layer; standalone-capable | Issued via LGA homesteader package, not a trial-onboarding bootjack. Runs on cached state when no node is in range — not "offline," just nothing to connect to. Reconciles gracefully on resync (no jarring dumps) |
| **Registration** | A QWST request needing pineal/QWAN delivery to complete | Claim staking specifically needs live nodes in range to "stick" — mechanical basis for claim jumping resolving in a settler's absence |
| **Presentation** | HUD exists — small, corner-of-vision status display (e.g. lower-left), detection blips, level/stat readouts | Not removed, *demoted*. Present and functional, but narrated the way a character glances at a dashboard gauge, not full-screen system-message spectacle. Stats are physically real, not just informational — see Sarah's snippet below |
| **QWST backronym** | Quantum Wideband Sensory Transduction | Chosen to abbreviate to "quest" as a pun; happens to also accurately describe the amygdala's function. Shared "Quantum Wide—" prefix with QWAN is intentional brand echo, not accidental layer-conflation (QWST doesn't touch the field directly) |
| **Physical stat expression** | QWST-linked stats affect the body directly, not just perception | Confirmed in prose: QWST-enabled strength and "level 3 child senses" (detection range) are real capability, not flavor text |
| **Child packages** | LGA/QWST issues governed packages for minors | Built-in safety governors cap physical stat expression (e.g. ~1.5× baseline strength for an 8-year-old) — a concrete, welcome worldbuilding detail from the snippet, worth treating as established |

## Geography

- **Twin-river country (west of the escarpment, around Nahal Junction):** Tigris-Euphrates-style twin rivers, settled first, denser, better land — ancient, unglaciated upper valley, mature forest. Exact compass orientation of the rivers themselves not yet confirmed — don't assert "north/south" without checking
- **Trunk-river backcountry (east of the escarpment, past Kitzigen Falls):** single Yukon-style trunk river, later/harsher claims, backcountry along tributaries — younger, glacier-scoured lower valley, patchier growth, exposed rock. **No maintained road reaches here** — the arterial road ends at Kitzigen Falls; the trunk river (shallow-draft — rafts and barges, not large ships, but real cargo capacity, distinct from the smaller canoe-only twin rivers upstream) is the actual transport backbone beyond it, with informal overland trails as the only alternative. Not enough traffic to justify a cut road
- **Green's Grant (corrected placement):** a grassland clearing in the *upper* reaches of Maple Valley, within the ancient upper-valley woodland — not the lower valley. Functions as a rest stop on the main arterial road between Nahal Junction and Kitzigen Falls. Maple Valley therefore spans both terrain ages: ancient woods at its headwaters (Green's Grant), younger glacier-scoured ground further down where the Ratkowski homestead sits
- **The two bounding ranges:** Himalayan/Karakoram-scale, running west-east, roughly parallel — the settled valley between them is genuinely wide, not a narrow pass, and is itself elongated west-east (matches west-to-east weather, far-west mega-lake, Nahal-Junction-west/Kitzigen-Falls-east layout)
- **The Escarpment:** **not** a mountain-range-scale feature itself (corrected) — a glacial scouring high-water mark cutting *across* the valley transversely (north-south), marking how far an ancient glacier advanced before stopping. Still produces real relief (cliffs, passes, the falls) via glacial plucking/scouring, just categorically a scour line, not a range. East-facing scarp. Three passes, north to south: **Schweddy Pass** (steep but manageable), **Kitzigen Falls** (easiest — the arterial road crosses here, and ends here — see below), **Yanike Pass** (nearly impassable, route of last resort). Full detail: [geography/climate-and-geology.md](../geography/climate-and-geology.md)
- **Nahal Junction:** capital, LGA seat, Cross terminus (Platform 1249) — **not** at the river confluence (corrected). Sits at the **western terminus** of the main arterial road, in the fertile land *between* the twin rivers — flanked by both, which is why it's still the best land and still fits the allotment-root naming (see Naming below). A second road T-intersects there, running **north-south**, reaching settlements on each river directly. **Kitzigen Falls** — the actual twin-river confluence, at the escarpment — is a separate location further **east** along the arterial road, consistent with the escarpment's established east-facing orientation
- **Climate hazard:** katabatic-style cold-snaps off the peaks — sudden, severe, distinct from ordinary winter, foreshadowable to old-timers not newcomers. Driven by an off-map mega-lake (Baikal + Great Lakes scale) as the valley's moisture source — also the reason this is forest-and-rivers country, not a barren cold-desert lee
- **The Winter Scale (four tiers):** No axial tilt — climate sits at a metastable ice-albedo balance point near the freeze threshold, sustained by the mega-lake's evaporation cycle. The feedback loop is a *brake on recovery, not a second engine* — it delays the reset, doesn't prevent it. **1 — White Fangs/Stormwall Burps:** common, 2–3 weeks, survivable with ordinary competence (see below). **2 — Normal Winter:** regular, 3–8 months, standard hard season — "winter" is settler terminology for sustained non-melting snow cover, not a claim about orbital mechanics; "regular" means homesteaders experience and plan around it reliably, not that it runs on a fixed calendar cycle **3 — Generational Winter:** once or twice a lifetime, a single severe event followed by winters that don't fully reset — real dramatic uncertainty, can break the loop or tip toward Scale 4. **4 — Geological Winter:** ~every 5,000 years, civilization-resetting, deep background only, never on the page. Full detail: [geography/climate-and-geology.md](../geography/climate-and-geology.md)
- **Stormwall Troughing / The White Fangs:** the dangerous version of the katabatic hazard has a mechanism and a direction — a jet-stream-analog system troughs down over **Stormwall** (the northern range), driving severe storms south through specific low passes *in Stormwall itself* (distinct from the three escarpment passes — Schweddy, Kitzigen Falls, Yanike — don't conflate them). Settler name for the resulting event: **the White Fangs** — a visible, advancing wall of ice-fog and flash rime (instant-rime is its own sharper danger, beyond generic cold) funneling through a known "fang-mouth" pass, giving anyone who reads the sign a narrow warning window. Deliberate, earned Jack London nod — mechanism worked out first, name second. Localized, not valley-wide: some claims sit in a known run-out path, some don't. Primary, expected, standing threat, can arrive anytime. The same off the **South Range** is a true anomaly — rare enough to catch even old-timers off guard. This asymmetry is why the north range is named (Stormwall) and the south isn't (plain "South Range") — deliberate, not unfinished. Full detail: [geography/climate-and-geology.md](../geography/climate-and-geology.md)
- One unpaved arterial road is the real backbone; ~6 trading posts serve as backcountry info hubs

## Naming

*Ya'ar* (forest) + *Nahal* (inheritance/hereditary allotment — root behind *nachalah*, tribal land divisions in the Hebrew Bible) = "forest grant." Wadi/river and allotment senses treated as one idea in-world: good land in an arid landscape *is* water-adjacent land, so Nahal Junction sitting at the river confluence isn't coincidental to its allotment-rooted name — it's the same logic twice. Structural/phonetic Semitic-echo per Hypatia's First Speech convention — texture and in-world folk etymology, not asserted real-world scholarship. First Speech deployment token: `'YRNHL` (proposed, unconfirmed).

## Ecology & Sky

- **Neomaples:** tappable, sap is a cross-species emergency food source — quiet irony against "nothing worth exploiting here"
- **The Cottage Oak** (*Quercus domesticus*, [ecology/cottage-oak.md](../ecology/cottage-oak.md)): corky, renewable, excavatable heartwood — settlers carve homes into it rather than build. Best shelter correlates with worst latitude; the LGA doesn't advertise this — a physical, concrete instance of its real-motive-vs-stated-motive pattern. Overharvest consequences arrive late (a tree stripped of cork can fail 1–2 winters later); uncured cork can't be burned safely (active deterrent compounds); structural-vs-fuel-cork tension is the go-to first-winter survival mechanic
- Yukon/mountain register: conifer forest, near-wolves, ash bears, trapline fur-bearer, corvid-analogs, one apex predator held in reserve as myth, not encounter
- Two suns — second is a distant white dwarf, ornamental, deliberate anti-Tatooine subversion (no heat, no eclipse, no narrative convenience)

## Settler Tracks

"Human" ≠ "Earther" — repo-seeded human populations exist on other Hypatia worlds too, diverged from Earth-stock, unconnected to Earth's Trial (see [races/homo/humans.md](../../../races/homo/humans.md)). Within the Earth-origin subset specifically:

1. **Economic settlers** — chose the LGA's offer deliberately, from anywhere in Hypatia including Earth
2. **Refugee-settlers** — displaced by Earth's Summer of Chaos (chaotic, wall-less disaster period predating the Commission). Family separation/fostering is the formative trauma; off-world placement to Ya'arnahal is a *later*, separate event, once Amoskeag/Cross terminus infrastructure exists. "Operation Pied Piper" is the tonal reference, not an in-world program name (yet)

**Protagonist** (working title *Little House on Maple Valley*) is track 2 — a solo emigrant claiming latecomer's ground below Nahal Junction, up a tributary off the trunk river. Instinctive, inarticulate purist-leaning stance on QWST/density integration, having already fled one totalizing infrastructure narrative (the Commission's) once.

## Tone

- **Place names are settler shorthand, not necessarily official — and that's deliberate, not unfinished.** Earthers are late arrivals to Ya'arnahal; leaning on Earth-culture-coded place names (a river called "the Pemigewasset" because that's the narrator's own mental shorthand) only works if it's framed as a substitute for an original/official name the narrator can't or won't render — the same mechanic First Speech already uses setting-wide for alien, unpronounceable system-facing labels (`'LGRNN`, `'THM`, `'YRNHL`), just applied at the geography level. This licenses every place name established so far (Kitzigen Falls, Schweddy Pass, Yanike Pass, Stormwall, Green's Grant, Maple Valley, etc.) to be read as settler/narrator naming rather than the "true" name. **No parallel official name needs to be invented for any of them** — leave that layer undefined until a specific scene actually needs it on the page, not before
- **Default to Earth-mundane; build out alien biology only when a scene needs it.** Fish are fish, most flora and fauna are unremarkable, unless a specific narrative purpose calls for something else — same deferral logic as the place-naming convention above, applied to ecology instead of geography. Cottage Oak, Lumberjacks, near-wolves, and ash bears all earned detail because they're doing real work (shelter, homesteading friction, or they showed up in actual prose); nothing else needs that treatment preemptively. Don't invent alien biology speculatively — wait for the story to ask for it
- **Emotional core: make the reader envy Sarah.** Unforgiving world, not despite it — competence-as-earned is the aspirational lever, comfort/safety is not. Craft check for any scene: does this read as real stakes she earned her way past, or misery for its own sake? The former is the goal; the latter works against it. Borrow *Call of the Wild*'s atmosphere (vastness, self-reliance, unmediated nature), not its tragic engine (identity loss, arbitrary tragedy). See [working-folder README — Emotional Core](../README.md) for the full framing
- Not dread — operational realism. The System's standalone/cached nature is always true everywhere; Ya'arnahal just has few enough nodes that it becomes visible and reasoned-about rather than imperceptible
- Not a dungeon-crawl — contrast case to Delverium
- **The HUD is present, not hidden — the correction is centrality, not visibility.** People who've lived with QWST their whole lives check it the way someone checks a mirror or a gauge: a glance, a blue dot, back to the conversation. The System isn't foregrounded with spectacle (no full-screen pop-ups interrupting a scene), but it's not bodily-only mysticism either — it has a literal on-screen presence, low-key
- **HUD vs. transduction is a false dichotomy — don't relitigate it.** The HUD is the visible symptom of the amygdala doing its job (sensory transduction rendering as a corner-of-vision overlay), not a competing claim about where the "real" system lives. A wideband sensory transduction system rendering as a HUD is just a plausible UI choice for how that transduction presents to a user — same relationship any HMD has to data it's gathering some other way. The actual authorial lever is plot-load-bearing vs. texture (quest markers driving the story vs. Sarah checking a HUD the way she'd check a mirror), not UI-present vs. UI-absent
- Native-displacement narrative deliberately excluded

## Confirmed from prose (`books/snippets/saras-open.md`)

- **Protagonist name: Sarah** (Ratkowski). Married/family homestead, not living solo at this point in the story — she has at least a daughter, Ann (age 8), and the text implies more family ("the rest of the family"). Reconcile with the "solo emigrant" arrival premise: she likely arrived alone as a refugee-settler and built a family since: arrival and the story's narrative present are not the same moment
- **Sarah's QWST is recent, not lifelong:** confirmed in prose — she lived roughly 20–30 years unenhanced before taking the LGA homesteader package as an adult. The Summer of Chaos displacement and the QWST enhancement are separated by decades — three life-stages (childhood displacement → decades baseline-human → adult enhancement at the grant), not a single continuous arc. "Her new strength" in the snippet is literal, not a general joke about nobody mastering it
- **Setting name: Maple Valley** — the specific valley where the Ratkowski homestead sits, within the Trunk River Backcountry, presumably up the tributary already established. This is the place the working title refers to, not a generic label
- **Named fauna:** near-wolves, ash bears — concrete names for what the world bible had as placeholder "wolf-analog" and unnamed megafauna/predator. Text confirms Maple Valley "might not have ravaging hordes of monsters, but it did have its share of wild animals" that would prey on a child — consistent with the original claim-jumper-fear-over-hordes design principle from early in this world's development
- **Bruin marshal:** Marshall Pelask is Bruin — the same race already established on Earth (`earth/AI-REFRESHER.md`: "independent authority... 'don't be a problem' over local law... authority is personal and reputational"). Consistent reuse: Bruin's personal/reputational-authority character fits a frontier marshal role even under a formal title. Worth treating as confirmation that established Hypatia races carry their characterization across worlds rather than being reinvented per-world
