# Hummingbee

**What it is:** A tiny, endothermic (warm-blooded), bird-like flyer, hummingbird-scaled and hummingbird-shaped, colored yellow and black. "Reptilian" describes visual/textural resemblance in its form — worth noting that under modern cladistics birds are technically classified within Reptilia anyway, so the label was never really a contradiction. What actually matters for the planet's exclusion rule is thermoregulation, not the taxonomic label: Hummingbee is endothermic, not an insect, and not an ectotherm of any kind.
**Category:** Fauna — Ya'arnahal, cold high latitudes
**Related:** [Ya'arnahal Glass](yaarnahal-glass.md), [Pemi Cattail](pemi-cattail.md), [The Winter Scale](climate-and-geology.md), [Ecology index](README.md)

## Naming

A settler's honest first impression — at a distance, easily mistaken for a large bumblebee; only on closer inspection does the true bird-like form become obvious (some scale-like or reptile-adjacent surface texture may contribute to the misread, but the creature itself is an endothermic bird-analogue, not any kind of ectotherm). The name stuck even after the mistake was corrected, in keeping with how most of Ya'arnahal's settler-given names work (see [AI Refresher — Tone](../meta/AI_REFRESHER.md)).

## Why Avian/Reptilian Rather Than Insectoid

**Corrected — it's ectothermy that's excluded globally, not reptile/insect lineages themselves.** Ya'arnahal's overall planetary energy balance is marginal (see [Planetary Geography](planetary-geography.md)) — there is no meaningfully warmer refuge anywhere on the planet robust enough to sustain *true ectothermic* life relying on ambient heat, not even at the equator. This isn't a cold-latitude-only restriction with a warmer off-map tropical refuge; there is no such refuge anywhere. But the exclusion targets the ectothermic *strategy*, not the lineage — a reptile-analogue that evolved genuine endothermy or specialized cold-adaptation is fair game and not ruled out by this. Whether the same allowance extends to insects is unconfirmed — don't assume it does. Endothermic analogues fill the niches ordinary ectotherms would occupy on a more temperate world: hummingbees for pollination and social-flyer niches, a vole/lemming-style small mammal for keystone-prey-base niches (not yet detailed — see [Ecology index](README.md)), and diverse fungi for decomposition. A small, warm-blooded, actively thermoregulating flyer being the pollinator here isn't a regional adaptation, it's the only kind of adaptation the planet's climate allows for an ordinary ectotherm to be replaced with.

## Ecological Role

Genuine nectar-feeders and pollinators, filling the niche bees occupy on Earth — not a narrative stand-in, but the same real ecological job (particularly suited to flower shapes a longer bill/tongue can access that a bee-equivalent couldn't). Likely drawn to [Pemi Cattail](pemi-cattail.md) and other flowering native flora.

## Social/Colonial Behavior

Unlike true hummingbirds (solitary, often aggressive, territorial on Earth), hummingbees have plausibly evolved toward cooperative, colonial behavior under two independent cold-climate pressures: shared thermal shelter-building (below) and cooperative or shared-knowledge foraging in a world where flowering/nectar availability is short and unpredictable rather than seasonally reliable. Both pressures point the same direction — toward cooperation being a better survival strategy here than solitary territoriality.

## The Winter Lantern (Shell-Building)

During cold cycles, colonies construct a thick, communal, clear casing around their shelter site to trap solar heat and seal out sub-zero cold — an especially fitting adaptation for a warm-blooded creature needing active thermal regulation to survive, more so than it would be for an insect that could otherwise simply go dormant. The casing is built in coordinated segments/courses over time by many individuals, producing natural structural seam lines between construction sessions — inherently weaker than the surrounding bulk material. (Third instance of the same underlying motif already established for [Cottage Oak](cottage-oak.md) and [Wicker Willow](wicker-willow.md): natural seams that are simultaneously a structural feature of the organism and the exact place a harvester — or here, the colony itself — exploits.)

## Emergence and Harvestable Seams

When conditions allow the colony to emerge, scout hummingbees gather extract from Pemi Cattail and apply it to the casing. Rather than dissolving the shell uniformly, the enzymatic extract preferentially penetrates and breaks down the weaker construction seams, fracturing the casing into discrete, structured plates rather than a formless gel — the colony escapes through a controlled crack pattern it can navigate, not a puddle. As the extract's moisture dries afterward, the reaction hyper-crosslinks and permanently vitrifies the material into a dense, glass-like, amber-tinted state — too hard for the colony to ever rework, which is why they abandon the old shell entirely and build fresh each cycle rather than reusing it.

## Mutualism with Settlers

Because the colony would otherwise have to clear or work around its own old casing before building anew, settlers removing discarded shells for their own use functionally does the colony a favor — neither species set out to help the other, but the relationship is genuinely, if unintentionally, cooperative. A regularly-harvested colony site may even prove more vigorous over successive seasons than an unharvested one, though this would only be the kind of thing a family notices after years of patient observation, not something anyone fully understands why.

## Settler Use

The abandoned casing, pre-structured along its own natural seams, is harvested closer to quarrying than to glassmaking from scratch — plates broken free along seam lines, then finished (softened at the edges, cast, cured) into the amber-tinted panes used in [Ya'arnahal Glass](yaarnahal-glass.md) glazing.
