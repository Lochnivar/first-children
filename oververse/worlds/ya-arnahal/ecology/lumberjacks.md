# Lumberjacks

**Species:** Gopher–jackrabbit cross (common name only — no scientific designation established)
**Category:** Fauna — Ya'arnahal, northern/upland latitudes
**Related:** [The Cottage Oak](cottage-oak.md), [Ecology index](README.md)

---

## What They Are

A gopher–jackrabbit cross, native to Ya'arnahal's northern/upland latitudes — the same range as [the Cottage Oak](cottage-oak.md). Rather than burrowing underground, lumberjacks tunnel into the tree's corky heartwood to hollow out den space, treating a Cottage Oak's core as an inverted, aboveground equivalent of a gopher hive.

## Behavior

Unlike a skilled human harvester, lumberjacks don't put the cork to any use — they simply remove it to clear space for their groves, the same way a gopher displaces dirt without regard for the dirt itself. The material is waste to them, not resource; whatever they clear gets discarded, typically piling up loose around the base of a colonized tree or nearby. Whether the tree survives their tunneling seems to be more a matter of scale than restraint — an individual colony likely doesn't remove material faster than the tree can regrow it, not because the lumberjacks are being careful, but because they simply don't take that much.

## Biology: Inedible by Bioaccumulation

Lumberjacks don't eat the cork, but living inside it isn't exposure-free — the Cottage Oak's deterrent compounds (the same ones that keep borers, grubs, and rot out; see [Cottage Oak — Three Adaptations](cottage-oak.md)) seep into their bodies through prolonged contact, not ingestion. They've evolved tolerance to survive constant exposure, but tolerance isn't the same as neutralizing it — the compound accumulates in their tissue over a lifetime. The result: lumberjacks are functionally inedible, to predators and to anyone considering them as game. No natural predation check on their numbers, and no consolation value to a settler already annoyed by them — unlike a nuisance animal that's at least worth trapping, a lumberjack costs a homesteader material and gives back nothing.

## Conflict with Settlers

Purely material and one-sided — not two harvesters competing for the same seam, but a homesteader trying to get usable cork out of a tree while an animal has been indifferently discarding perfectly good material to clear den space, with no regard for its worth to anyone else. That wasted value is what actually makes them aggravating rather than merely inconvenient. A colonized tree is fairly easy to spot even for a green claimant — the telltale scatter of cast-off cork debris around the base doesn't require the kind of hard-won reading skill that judging a tree's overall health does (see [Cottage Oak — Harvest and Husbandry](cottage-oak.md)).

## Tone

A low-key, recurring nuisance rather than a threat — day-to-day homesteading friction, not danger. Consistent with the founding design principle for this world's wildlife: claim-jumper fear over hordes, ordinary friction over spectacle. Best delivered in dialogue without explanation — *"Damn it Bob, them Lumberjacks are back in the Oaks again!"* — the exclamation alone carries the worldbuilding. The name's irony (a small burrowing animal named after the human logging profession, despite treating wood as pure obstacle rather than resource) is best left unremarked-on in-world — funny only from the reader's outside vantage, same restraint already earned by the White Fangs' Jack London nod.
