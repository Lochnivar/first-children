## Overview

Ya'arnahal is a frontier land-run world opened by the Land Grants Authority (LGA), operating out of Mercara's Cross. A "cold rush" — no treasure resource; the land grant itself, bundled with a QWST homesteader package, is the draw, while the Authority's real motive is mesh densification and distributed prospecting, dressed as generosity and opened to all races at once to avoid the appearance of favoritism. Tonal target: *Little House on the Prairie* domestic competence fused with *Call of the Wild* / *White Fang* isolation — the System present as quiet, mostly-invisible structure rather than genre spectacle.

**A working reference for the Ya'arnahal novel(s), working title *Little House on Maple Valley*.**

## Core Concepts

- **The LGA:** Land Grants Authority, Cross-chartered, three-part motive (mesh density, distributed prospecting, preemptive multi-race opening)
- **The "cold rush":** survival is the stakes, not treasure
- **The System:** QWAN (pineal, range-limited node broadcast) + QWST (amygdala, standalone-capable, cache-and-reconcile) — visible on Ya'arnahal only because density is low, not because it's a different system
- **Claim registration:** a network operation — the mechanical basis for claim jumping resolving in a settler's absence
- **Nahal Junction:** capital, LGA seat, Cross terminus (Platform 1249), named for the land-grant/allotment root, not the river it happens to sit on
- **The protagonist:** solo Earth-refugee emigrant, Summer-of-Chaos displacement track, claiming latecomer's ground below the Junction

## Emotional Core

**Make the reader envy Sarah.** Not envy a soft life — Ya'arnahal doesn't forgive, and the place will not notice if you stop — envy earning a real life on an alien world through your own competence. The aspirational lever is competence-as-earned, not comfort or safety: a reader should finish a scene wanting this, *because* the stakes were real and she was equal to them, not in spite of the stakes.

Borrow *Call of the Wild*'s atmosphere — vastness, self-reliance, unmediated nature — without its tragic engine (identity loss, arbitrary tragedy, moral compromise as the point). Real-world touchstone: the same appetite that draws an audience to authentic off-grid/homesteading content — people don't want it faked-safe, they want to watch someone earn their way through something real.
