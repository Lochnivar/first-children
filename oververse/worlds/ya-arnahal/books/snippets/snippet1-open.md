**Chapter One**

I'm sure the river has an official name that's recorded on some Land Grants Authority maps, but it's unpronounceable by this human's tongue. So I'm just going to call it the Pemigewasset, because it is almost identical to its namesake back home. Granted, Pemigewasset is a mouthful itself, but no self-respecting Granite Stater would call it anything other than "the Pemi." So, our new home in Ya'arnahal gets its own Pemi.

This Pemi could have been grafted directly from New Hampshire, at least the stretches that I can see from what we've claimed as the Moreau Homestead. It was a shallow, wide waterway lined with stone, some gravel-sized ranging up to honest boulders. The banks were gently sloped, and there was a nice gravel bar that the kids love wading from.

"You're going to be sore in the morning," Sarah says as she flops into the chair beside me. It was a matching pair, rough-hewn Adirondack-style lounge chairs. They were made of some native wood that we called Wicker Willow. It was surprisingly strong without an obvious drop in durability.

"You got that right," I said as I rolled my left arm in the shoulder. It was my left shoulder before the QWST upgrades had repaired the old torn rotator cuff.

"But I guess it's a good thing that Logan is becoming such a beast.  He can really help with the lifting.  I dropped my arm to run my fingers through the river gravel. "And both Lucas and Jesse are able to help with smaller tasks."

Sarah's face took on a grumpy cast at the mention of Lucas doing any kind of risky work. Lucas was her baby, even at fourteen Earth years old, and she had fallen in love with the little guy the moment he came to us through foster care.

"I know you're worried about him, Love," I reached over and grasped her left hand. "But you can't keep him wrapped up forever."

~Especially when there's nobody but us to keep the job alone~ I didn't say the thought out loud.

"I know," Sarah sighed, "but I can't help the way I feel."

I took a drink of my own mapleade to keep my own thoughts to myself.

"Anyways, you're keeping a close eye on him and Jesse?  Those two feed off each other."

"Yep," I said. "I let a small amount of exasperation slip into my voice. As much as I can while still getting the house up.  The Cottage Oak isn't growing fast enough to give us the space need."

Sarah grimaced. She really hated living in the temporary home we had literally excavated from what we called a Cottage Oak. Like some oaks back home, the Cottage Oak grew a corky material, only the Cottage Oak grew the cork on the inside, in the heartwood core, not the bark like the ones on Earth.

It made for a relatively easy house, once you got the cork out you needed to make rooms. There were rigid internal support structures like beams that made natural dividers and floors. An afternoon's work on a relatively large Cottage Oak made for a cozy shelter for a family of four.

The problem was the Moreau family was a family of eight. Two adults, four of their own kids, and two more that had been adopted otherwise. It was complicated.

"I hate that place," Sarah said as she wrinkled her nose. "It reeks."

I dimly nodded, agreeing whole heartedly.

"Speaking of the kids," she changed the subject. "Where are they?"

My grin returned as I risked my life through snark.

"If you had your own HUD, you'd know"

Sarah glared daggers at me that did surprisingly did little damage against my armor of snark.  I checked my HUD and noted the six blue dots along with my own white dot, typological overlays helped me orient myself.

"Logan is about half a mile upstream, probably fishing. Lucas is in the house..."

"Hopefully doing his homework" she groused.

"Hopefully doing his homework," I agreed.  "Lauren is about three-quarters of a mile south with Leah and Charlotte, probably gathering milknuts and berries.  Jesse is about a quarter mile across the river.  He wanted to spend his evening hacking out some coork for the next layer of logs.  We were running a bit low." 

Sarah sighed.

"I'm worried about him," she said.  "He was a wild one, but he's changed since Sheryl died and I'm not sure it's in a good way."

I could only agree.  Jesse had been another foster child brought into our lives when his foster mother brought him into her home when he was only a few weeks old.  Sheryl was a friend of Sarah's so Jesse had joined our expanded group of friends.

He had grown up a bit wild, one who could shift from petty and cruel, like most kids, to caring and generous in the blink of an eye.  Had he been able to grow up on Earth, I think he would have grown out of the childish selfishness.  But, here?  In a new world?  Alone and disconnected from everybody?

"We'll work with him.  Have some faith, Love."  I squeezed her hand in reassurance and she look at me with doubt.

"You know I'm not a big believer in God, Nick."

"That's ok, I'll believe in Him enough for both of us."  I smiled.  "Besides, I'm not talking about God in this one.  Just our skills and how resilent a child can be.  Have faith in us."

Sarah nodded and took another sip and let the quiet sit.  We faced north and the Stormwall stretched across the horizon, taller than any mountain range on Earth.  We least, we think they are taller.  Nobody has climbed them yet or measured them to the best of my knowledge.  But, for all the distance, over a hundred or more miles, they still loomed.

The primary sun was beginning to set and we could see the line of red begin to creep along the mountain range from east to west.  It was slanted diagonally up the mountains and the white snow faded to blood red then finally to black.

I looked up and smiled at the wide band of rings that arced across the sky.  They shimmered as tumbling peices of ice reflected the sun from over the horizon and the fractured glare traced random fragments of light across the ground.  

Neither of Ya'arnahal's small moons were in the sky right now and the stars were starting to come out as the sky dampened from blue to black.  The Everstar was rising in the east and the air was starting to chill noticably as the heat from the sun vanished behind the western horizon.

Sarah shivered as a particularly chilly breeze ruffled her hair and she wrapped her arms around her.  I noticed and drained the last of my drink.  I handed her the glass.

"I'll go get Jesse, can you take that in for me?  The girls look like they're almost back to the tree."

"You could just send him a message through your HUD," Sarah reminded me.

"True."  I acknowledged.  "But, he might need a hand with bringing the cork in."

"Or the rocks that distracted him."

"Hey, a boy's got to have his distraction.  Logan has his fishing, Lucas loves Battletech and Jesse loves his rocks."

"And your distraction?"  Sarah raised an eyebrow.

"You, of course."  I grinned and pulled her close for a kiss.  "Besides, he needs to know that we care enough to come for him instead of just sending a message."

I gave her one last winked and bent to pick up the crossbow that I had been keeping beside my chair.  You never knew about the wildlife.  The water was cold and came up to my mid-calves, but the rocky bottom made for sure footing.
