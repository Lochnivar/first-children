# Chapter Two

The crunching of my footsteps on the ground fell into silence as I climbed the easy north bank and stepped into the rainbow heather on the north bank.   Like the earth plant we had named it after, it was a low brush-like flowering plant only it's blooms were slightly larger and colored in all colors of the rainbow.  We didn't know why they created swathes of colors, some plants even had multiple colors on the same stalk.  They seemed to be the same species.

I admired the technicolored wonder among the green scrub and calf-high grasses.  Even in the darkening evening, the colors were brilliant, almost luminous.  For a hundred yards, the clearing stretched, the trees giving the river its distance on both sides.

As I reentered the trees, the canopy cut the fading light into near total darkness.  I switched my vision into low-light, another gift of the HUD that QWST had provided to us.  Fallen leaves and a low ground cover plant similar to a creeping ivy carpeted the floor beneath the canopy. Occasionally my foot would find a branch and, if it were dry enough, the sharp crack would be the only sound my boots made.

As I looked around, I was amazed at how familiar this place felt. Generations of scifi authors had dreamed of the spectrum, the absolute plethora of alien life, both flora and fauna... everything from Green Mars to Avatar's Pandora, I had read or watched everything I could get my hands on. I had half imagined something out of the Lorax when the LGA had offered us Ya'arnahal.

Generations of scifi authors would have wept in disappointment at how utterly prosaic the reality was. If you didn't look too closely, the woods around our homestead could have been any woodlands in New England or Oklahoma, where I had grown up. If you closed your eyes, you still heard the sounds of nature.

I paused at a few neomaples, or just maples as we call them, to check the sap buckets.  The LGA data in the HUD says that the sap has some nutrition rather than just pure sugar.  But, the LGA data has been known to be wrong before, so I wanted to check the output and the health of the tree before I invest too much into it.  So far, the yield seemed to be accurate, a lot more on this one tap than you'd get from an Earth maple and it doesn't look to be harming the tree.  I'll need to test boil a few of these to see exactly how they react.  

"Note to self," I murmurred.  "Grab the bucket on the way home."  

The HUD dinged quietly in my head, confirming the note had been recorded.  I shook my head in irritation.   My 'Notes to Self" was a habit I had had since I was a child, it helped me remember things.  But, the HUD had picked up and actually asserted it as a literal note to keep.  

That worried me in more ways than I wanted to admit.  The idea of the HUD was a sci-fi dream come true, straight out of every LitRPG book I had read.  What worried me was my coming dependence on it.  It was crucial in helping us adapt to this world, but if it began supplanting things like basic memory or task management.

That was a worry for future Nick and I focused on Jesse's blue dot as I headed deeper into the forest.  I heard the muffled chipping before I saw the tree and smiled.  From the speed of the chopping, I assumed that Jesse was making up for lost time.  He had seen my dot on his own HUD and panicked.

As the tree came into view, I saw the wicker willow basket that he had brought to carry the cork home in.  The basket had plenty of stones and rocks in it, but very little actual cork.  I smiled.  Truly, I had yet to find a ten-year old that could keep their focus on a boring task for long.  I was more worried about the speed that Jesse was working at.  If he didn't slow down, he'd take a foot off with the cork scoop.

"Jesse", I called through the opening he had created.  The caustic musky smell of the cork was strong and I hope Jesse had remembered the face covering we typically used  to keep the flying cork dust out of our mouths and noses.  

"One moment!"  A final, solid twack of the scoop and a shower of cork through the hole, into the basket before Jesse's feet popped out a few minutes later.  Of course, he hadn't remembered the mask.

"The cork oil is poisonous if it gets in your mouth."  I admonished with a smile, just enough worry to get my point across, but enough warmth that it didn't sting too hard.

"I'm sorry Mr. Moreau," he said, "I mean Nick, sorry".

"As long as you didn't swallow any of it."
