## Scale

**Planet:** Roughly Earth-sized, slightly larger.

**Axial tilt:** Near-zero — no true seasonal cycle (already established; see [Base Condition](climate-and-geology.md)). Climate is instead a function of **latitude** and **elevation**, not season. This doesn't eliminate the temperature gradient a tilted planet would have — equatorial regions still receive more direct solar radiation than polar regions on any spherical planet, tilt or not. What's absent is *seasonal* variation superimposed on that gradient: the equator is reliably mild (relatively speaking), with no winter/summer distinction of its own.

**Global climate character:** Marginal, planet-wide — Ya'arnahal sits close to a global ice-albedo tipping point (see [The Feedback Risk](climate-and-geology.md), now understood as a planetary-scale mechanism, not just a valley-local one). Even the equatorial band, the planet's warmest available latitude, is only barely, precariously survivable by Earth standards. Polar regions are correspondingly extreme: truly massive, planet-dominating ice caps, likely uninhabitable outright.

**Scale nesting — three distinct scopes, not one:**

1. **On-map settled region:** Alaska-scale (~1.7 million km²) — the LGA's actual land-run footprint, a frontier that's been opened but is nowhere near fully settled.
2. **The valley-and-mountain system:** Roughly Siberia-scale (~13 million km²) — settled region plus its off-map extensions (the far side of the mountains, the full glacial trough, the mega-lake's coastline). Chosen deliberately over a China-scale comparison, since Siberia's character (vast, cold, sparse, river- and frontier-defined) matches the setting's tone as well as its scale. Most of this footprint is carried by east-west sprawl rather than north-south spread: the glacial trough swings hundreds of miles east before curving back north — this is *why* the escarpment faces east despite ranges massive enough to redirect glaciation from the north (already established in [climate-and-geology.md — Regional Structure](climate-and-geology.md); this is what actually explains it). The mega-lake sits far to the west as the system's climate-driving moisture source.
3. **The continent this system sits within:** Roughly Asia+Europe combined in landmass — the valley-and-mountain system above is just one region of it, not the whole thing. Surrounded by oceans and other continents at the planetary scale. **This outer layer is deliberately, probably permanently unbuilt** — "we may never get to it," full stop, not a placeholder awaiting future development. Don't let scope creep upward past the valley system without a specific reason.

The Siberia-scale figure is the system's total footprint, not the planet's total land — keep these distinct. Consistent with "the world is big, the story is here."

## Latitude Placement

- **Southern bounding range:** ~10°N.
- **The Stormwall** (northern bounding range): its southern face sits at roughly ~25–35°N, extending north from there toward the pole. It **actively holds back a vast reservoir of the planet's polar cold** — not just "the mountains that make storms," a literal climatic barrier. White Fangs are **breaches in that barrier**: cold air escaping through specific passes, not ordinary regional weather. This raises the stakes of the whole Stormwall/White Fang system — it isn't generating weather so much as failing to fully contain something much larger and worse.
- **Maple Valley / the settled region:** sits in the gap between the two ranges, roughly ~15–20°N — **genuinely near-equatorial, the planet's mildest available band.** This is a significant reframe worth internalizing: the settled region isn't "a cold place near the pole," it's the *best* climate this planet has to offer, and it's still this harsh. There is no warmer refuge to dream of reaching — this is as good as it gets.

**Why Maple Valley is livable at all:** Not simply "equatorial = warm enough" — the valley is a specifically sheltered pocket between two major mountain systems, each doing real climatic work. The southern range's exact function (mirrored cold-bank, different regime, or simply unmapped) is still open — not yet decided, don't assume symmetry with the Stormwall.

## Elevation vs. Latitude Within the Valley

Local severity scales with elevation and proximity to the Stormwall specifically, layered on top of the valley's latitude: **upper valley** (ancient, unglaciated, closer to the Stormwall, higher elevation) reads *colder*; **lower valley** (younger, glacier-scoured, lower elevation, farther from the Stormwall) reads comparatively *milder* — the same elevation/proximity logic that governs planetary habitability, operating again at valley-internal scale.

**Flag, not silently resolved:** this is a *temperature* claim, and it sits alongside an existing *terrain/infrastructure* claim that the lower valley is "harsher" (rougher ground, no maintained road, later-settled — see [climate-and-geology.md — The Escarpment](climate-and-geology.md) and [Trunk River Backcountry](../locations/regions/trunk-river-backcountry.md)). These are different axes, not a contradiction — the lower valley can be *milder in raw temperature* while still being *harsher in terrain and infrastructure* — but future writing should be careful not to conflate "harsher" (settlement difficulty) with "colder" (temperature), since they now point in different directions. Cottage Oak's cold-hardiness making it a fixture of the "northern/upland" zone is consistent with this: it fits the colder upper valley specifically, not a claim about the planet's poles.

## Ectothermy Exclusion (Global, Strategy-Level Not Lineage-Level)

Given how marginal the planet's overall energy balance is, there is no meaningfully warmer refuge anywhere on Ya'arnahal robust enough to sustain *true ectothermic* life relying on ambient heat — not even at the equator, and not restricted to cold latitudes with a warmer off-map refuge elsewhere; there is no such refuge anywhere. **This excludes the ectothermic strategy, not reptile or insect lineages as such.** Reptiles are fine to exist here as long as they're endothermic or specially cold-adapted — the planet rules out relying on ambient heat, not the taxonomic category. Whether the same allowance extends to insects is unconfirmed; don't assume it without checking.

**The mechanism is a hard reproductive bottleneck, not soft competitive disadvantage.** Ya'arnahal's biosphere and climate bottleneck ectothermic evolution at the individual level: the vast majority of any lineage reverting toward ectothermy simply doesn't survive to the next generation the first time that reversion is expressed. This isn't ectotherms losing out gradually to better-adapted endotherms over many generations — it's a filter so hard that a reversion essentially never gets the chance to persist or re-establish itself once endothermy is locked into a lineage. That's what makes the exclusion this total rather than just heavily favored. Endothermic analogues fill the niches ordinary ectotherms would occupy on a more temperate world: [Hummingbee](../ecology/hummingbee.md)s for pollination/social-flyer niches, a vole/lemming-style small mammal for keystone-prey-base niches (not yet detailed), and diverse fungi for decomposition.

## Sky

In addition to the two suns already established (see [climate-and-geology.md — Sky](climate-and-geology.md)):

**Ring system:** Equatorial, aligned with the shepherd moons' orbital plane — the dynamically stable default, since an equatorial ring doesn't fight the planet's oblateness-driven precession the way an inclined ring would. Viewed from Maple Valley's near-equatorial latitude, the ring arcs dramatically high across the sky rather than sitting low on the horizon — no artificial tilt required, it falls out naturally from the valley's own equatorial position.

**The Gap:** A permanent, visible gap in the ring — settler folklore attributes it to something having "burned through," origin otherwise unconfirmed/unexplained in-world. Serves as the primary calendar/timekeeping marker, recurring roughly monthly, unambiguous in a way the two small shepherd moons (easily confused with each other) are not.

**Shepherd moons:** Two small moons, not yet individually detailed, sharing the ring's orbital plane.
