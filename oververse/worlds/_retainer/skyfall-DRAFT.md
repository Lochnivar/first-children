## One-Line Premise

A world the **Greater Dragons built for themselves and their Lesser Dragon children**, which **First Children later infiltrated** — seeding their repository species there. Those species' mages built competing magical **dungeons** that got out of control, spawned monsters uncontrollably, and overran the ground. The survivors now live in **floating magi-tech cities** and travel by **airship**, raiding the uninhabitable surface for resources. Only the skies are safe.

---

## The Catastrophe (Origin of the Now)

- **Cause:** Competing mage factions / schools / orders (FC-repository species) created their own magical dungeons.
- **Trigger:** Some form of **sabotage / foul play** — but the decisive failure was **critical mass**. The number of dungeons exceeded any ability to contain them; spawned monsters swamped all containment efforts.
- **Open intent question (see below):** Was the sabotage meant to cause exactly this, or did someone unleash something they believed containable that cascaded past intent? (Leaning toward cascade-past-intent for richer present-day politics, but **not yet decided**.)
- **Why nobody just destroyed the dungeons at the source:** Overwhelmed, not incompetent — too many, too fast. Containment was swamped, not skipped.
- **Status of the apocalypse now:** **Settled grim equilibrium**, not an ongoing worsening spike. People have built lives around the sky-only reality.

---

## The Now (Present-Day Status Quo)

- Survivors live **primarily** on floating cities ("primarily" leaves room for other enclaves — see POV note).
- Travel by **magi-tech airships**.
- The ground is uninhabitable due to monster hordes. **Raids** descend for resources; **settlement attempts are overrun and destroyed.**
- **Why raids work where settlements don't:** Monsters are **slow to mobilize, not weak.** Raids are in-grab-out before the monsters can rally and respond. Even so, raiding is genuinely risky.

### Raiding as a Structure
- Not purely opportunistic. Raids range from **window-based** (wait for a relatively safe period, grab what's available) to **goal-based** (we need this specific thing now, which changes/ignores the "safe" timing).
- Goal-based raids shift the risk calculus — they accept danger or manufacture temporary safety. (Exact mechanism — higher casualties vs. force-projection/perimeter-clearing — **not yet decided**; this could map onto faction wealth/capability differences between cities.)

### Tech Level
- Goal: **as low-tech as possible.** Likely resolution: the floating cities and core float-mechanism are **inherited dragon-grade infrastructure** the survivors barely maintain rather than invented — the one "high" concession sits in the origin, while the mortals are scavenger-maintainers. (**Not yet confirmed** — alternative is survivors engineered the cities themselves post-collapse. See Open Questions.)

## Point of View / Scope Discipline

- **The sky-dwellers only know about the sky-dwellers.** Limited POV is deliberate.
- Other survivor enclaves (ground bunkers, nomads, stranger things) are intentionally left unresolved — to be revealed during storytelling as a twist, not explained up front. Consistent with "raid fast and leave" (raiders have no reason to encounter anyone else).

## The Dragon Layer

### Greater Dragons
- **Built this world** for themselves and their Lesser Dragon children.
- **Not involved in the present.** Offstage (consistent with the universe's general creator-goes-offstage pattern). Per genus.md they cannot be permanently destroyed — absence now does not require death, just departure.
- **Open:** Did they simply leave after building it, or did something happen to them in/around the catastrophe? (**Not yet decided.**)

### Lesser Dragons
- **Still present but population-limited.** Ground down by **three-front attrition**: monsters, human/FC-repository species, and **each other**.
- This explains "few left" without a separate cause.

### East/West Dichotomy + Infighting (NEW — being developed)
- Inject an **Eastern vs. Western dragon** cultural/morphological split among Lesser Dragons, with active fighting between the factions.
- Proposed grounding in existing canon: lineage is already determined by Greater Dragon parent, and color already carries lineage meaning (chromatic = aggressive, metallic = cooperative). So East/West can be **lineage-deep**: different Greater Dragon parents built their children on different templates —
  - **Western:** winged, territorial, fire, "burn it all / the monsters can be fought."
  - **Eastern:** serpentine, weather/sky-tied, wisdom-coded, "the plague is weather, you don't fight it, you endure/redirect it."
- **The hard question still open (must resolve):** Why do the last dragons kill each other while monsters eat everyone? Shared extinction usually suppresses infighting, so the war needs a reason strong enough to override "we are all about to die."
  - **Proposed strongest framing (fits the universe's inherited-grievance grain):** The war is an **argument *about* the apocalypse** neither side can concede — each blames the other for how the collapse went (e.g., one faction bordered the dungeon-cradles and says the other abandoned them; or they fundamentally disagree on whether the monsters can be fought vs. endured). Neither can back down without admitting their dead died for nothing.
- **Unresolved fork:** Is the East/West split **pre-catastrophe lineage** (ancient, the plague merely inflamed it) or **catastrophe-born** (same dragons, fractured by how to face the end — raw, still-bleeding)? **Not yet decided.**

## Continuity / Framework Hooks

- **FC infiltration angle:** This fits the established pattern where FC create/seed something to prove a point or expand reach without fully reckoning with consequences (cf. Dracari, the Greater-Dragon/FC taxonomic grudge). Here, FC seeded repo species into a world that wasn't theirs, and those species broke it.
- **Grievance rhymes:** Greater Dragons opposing/resenting FC is already canon (genus.md: "Willing to oppose FC"). A dragon-built world wrecked by an FC-species sideshow is a natural extension of that sibling tension.
- **Distinct from Delverium:** Delverium is the gamified LitRPG dungeon world (Zorathar's meritocracy experiment). **This world is explicitly NOT LitRPG** — its dungeons are a catastrophic, accidental industrial-scale disaster, not a game. Keep the contrast sharp so the two "dungeon worlds" don't blur.
