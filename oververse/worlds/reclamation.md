# The Reclamation *(working title — name is a placeholder)*

**Status:** Concept development — framework-consistent, not yet drafted into prose
**Category:** Worlds / Cross-World Event
**Related:** [Earth](earth/trial-mechanics.md), [Iolanu](iolanu.md), [First Children](../cosmology/first-children.md), [World Creation](../mechanics/world-creation.md), [QWAN](../mechanics/qwan.md), [Monsters and Rifts](iolanu/world-building/natural-world/monsters_and_rifts.md), [Greyroses](iolanu/world-building/organizations/greyroses.md), [Earth Souls](iolanu/characters/groups/earth_souls.md), [Soul Transfer](../mechanics/soul-transfer.md)

## Overview

Iolanu was never a legitimate creation — Malachor (Zeta) ripped it off as a bootleg copy of Earth's "repo entry" thousands of years ago, rearranging the geography and claiming it against peer judgment. After Malachor is defeated/driven off at the conclusion of the Gabby/Apostasy arc, the First Child who actually created Earth — the same one who activated Earth's HiRB field and then walked away from the resulting mess — returns for the first time since the original QWAN activation. Not to fix anything. To take back what was stolen from him.

He forces an auto-merge: Iolanu, as an illegitimate fork, gets folded back into its source. He does this the way FC do everything — carelessly, without curation, without regard for what's downstream. It happens roughly ten years into Earth's Trial (see [Earth trial-mechanics.md](earth/trial-mechanics.md)), after the wall system, the Commission, and Earth's whole fragile equilibrium have had a decade to stabilize.

This is not a new "Book" created from scratch. It's the resulting state after a forced, botched merge commit — full sibling to Rakaiah's "abandoned experiment nobody chose" tone and Stratara/Hegemon's "collaboration devolved into something nobody controls," but structurally closer to **Hegemon-Stratara Connection** in that it exists to document an interaction between two already-established worlds rather than standing alone. Difference: Hegemon-Stratara is two worlds staying separate and connected. This is one-way absorption — Iolanu stops being an independent Book.

**Authorial note:** This file documents the delta, not the baseline. Iolanu's files and Earth's files up to this point remain the authoritative pre-merge record and are not edited to anticipate this event. Reference them; don't restate them here.

## Why "Reclamation," Not "Merge"

From the FC's side, this isn't a merge in any considered sense — he's not reconciling two versions of something, he's taking his property back. "Auto-merge" is the mechanical description of *how* it happens (the git framing, useful for reasoning about which content survives), but the FC's own motive is simpler and less interested: this is repossession, not reunification. He would not recognize "merge" as a meaningful description of what he's doing.

## Trigger and Timing

- **Same FC** who activated HiRB on Earth (see [trial-mechanics.md §1](earth/trial-mechanics.md)) — not a new character. This is the first time an FC who touched Earth has returned since walking away from the original crisis. He isn't returning to explain, fix, or acknowledge the Commission's decade of cleanup. He's running an unrelated errand against his idiot sibling and Earth is where the stolen property currently is.
- **Timing:** ~10 years into Earth's Trial. Long enough that the wall system, Gatelock economy, RV wagon corridor culture, and integration metric have become settled, lived-in normal — this disrupts an already-stabilized equilibrium rather than compounding the original chaos.
- **Precondition on the Iolanu side:** Happens after the Gabby/Apostasy arc concludes and Malachor is defeated/driven off. Nala's own liberation plan (evacuate souls, rebuild a plants-and-animals-only Iolanu — see [world-creation.md](../mechanics/world-creation.md)) is superseded by this event, not fulfilled by it. **Nala does not intervene to stop it and is not framed as trying to.** Her actual goal — grieving Malachor in his own element — is already accomplished by the time this happens. She does not care about the systemic outcome. Whether she personally extracts anyone she cares about before the merge closes is an open, deliberate authorial choice (see Open Threads).

## The Merge Algorithm

The FC's process is mechanical and semantically blind — it does not evaluate importance, meaning, or narrative weight. This is the source of most of the "ugly."

**Rule: no Earth equivalent = clean apply. Earth equivalent exists = Earth wins.**

- **Rifts:** Earth has nothing resembling a quantum rift. Zero conflict. They come over as pure collateral damage — not a deliberate transplant, but the wound left by tearing content out of one substrate and stapling it into another (parallel to the Shattering's own origin as ancient Quantumancers tearing reality). See [monsters_and_rifts.md](iolanu/world-building/natural-world/monsters_and_rifts.md) for the underlying mechanic (Main Rift, mobile rifts, congregation signal behavior) — all of it applies unchanged on Earth's side.
- **Homo Quantus:** Genetically drifted humans — technically still Homo sapiens, modified by Malachor during his rule of Iolanu (frame as genetic drift, not a separate species). No Earth equivalent, so they merge clean, as a population. **Cultures within Homo Quantus (Thohalla, Caelori, Veylori, Wrinomo, Kitzengai, Rathen, etc.) are not separately-conflicting content — they are subcultures of the same no-conflict population, and survive intact as living cultures, not erased.** Thohalla specifically arrive as a landless people whose entire civilizational purpose — riftwalking, mobile-rift sealing, congregation-outrunning caravan discipline — becomes immediately and desperately relevant, since Earth has no institutional experience with the exact threat that just arrived with them.
- **Geography and place-bound institutions:** Iolanu's continents were already "rearranged from the source" — they conflict directly with Earth's real geography and lose. Cities are resolved individually, not as a wholesale map: tag each by lat/long. If an inhabited Earth city occupies that coordinate, Earth wins and the Iolanu city does not survive. If the coordinate is empty or the Earth location is uninhabited, the Iolanu city comes over intact. Since Iolanu's geography was never a strict 1:1 map of Earth to begin with, placement can be arbitrary — there's no obligation to place a surviving city "where it would have been" on the source map.
  - This produces real, uncurated unfairness: a civilizationally important Iolanu location can be erased for the sole reason that a real Earth town happens to occupy that coordinate, while an unimportant village survives purely because it landed on empty tundra. **This arbitrariness should not always break in the story's favor** — resist the pull to let "the cities we care about" always conveniently survive. The mechanic is only as sharp as its willingness to be unfair to the plot, not just to Iolanu.
  - Surviving cities may land in terrain that doesn't match their original design (mountain fortress on flat prairie, port city with no ocean) — free texture, no cost.
  - The same tag-and-check logic likely extends to other geography-scale items (mountain ranges, the Main Rift's landing point, sacred sites) — not yet resolved; treat per-item, on demand, rather than pre-building a full merged atlas.

## Consequences on Earth

- **Containment is accidental, not designed.** The wall system was built to enforce administrative-boundary integration testing, not to contain monsters. It turns out to do the job anyway — dwarf-grown, QWAN-active walls are arguably *better* suited to containing "living quantum noise" than Iolanu's own masonry city walls ever were. A rift landing inside a room stays bounced around inside that room's boundary; corridors, being separately walled from rooms, keep functioning right past a sealed-off, monster-infested county.
- **Leakage happens through seams that already exist**, not new lore: [Known Seams §9.1 of trial-mechanics.md](earth/trial-mechanics.md) — the Amoskeag canal/waterway classification ambiguity (Seam One, sitting directly under Commission HQ — real irony potential) and unpatched cave/grown-wall intersections (Seam Two, worst in the North Country, Carroll County, and other thin-survey rural areas). Monsters actively seek these out rather than stumbling into them — the existing congregation/sapience-signal mechanic already establishes that monsters which plan, wait, and adapt generate a stronger signal and are the more dangerous variant; a monster trapped against a wall with a town's population as the nearest signal is going to behave exactly like that.
- **The integration metric becomes a liability in rift-contained rooms.** Walls dissolve at ~90% integration and reform at ~50% (see [trial-mechanics.md §5.1](earth/trial-mechanics.md)). A room that's trapped a rift has an existential incentive to *suppress* its own integration score — cooperation and mixed traffic across that specific boundary becomes something residents sabotage on purpose, inverting the incentive every other room on Earth is working toward. This falls directly out of combining two already-established mechanics; it isn't a new rule.
- **The Commission's KPI/trial narrative is not destroyed by this — it's ignored, which is worse.** The FC isn't attacking the Commission's cover story; he almost certainly doesn't know it exists, and wouldn't care if he did. The narrative may well survive intact, patched over with the same institutional muscle memory that built it in the first place ("there's a form for it"). The interesting question isn't whether the fiction survives — it does, functionally — it's whether the Commission's habit of institutionalizing catastrophe (see the "Jeff-class incident" pattern, [first-children.md](../cosmology/first-children.md)) can actually metabolize something this large, or whether this is the one that's too big for a form number.
- **Homo Quantus break the Commission's taxonomy at the root.** They're not a new alien species slotting into an existing non-human category (independent contractor, wild race, Homo genus staff, Orcus adversarial deployment) — they're humans, by every metric the Commission's KPIs use, who are also now permanently different from other humans, with real ability. That's a crack in the human/alien binary the entire wall-and-KPI system assumes, not just a new population to process.

## Open Threads (deliberately unresolved)

- **Permanent name.** "The Reclamation" is a placeholder — captures the FC's motive (taking back stolen property) rather than a neutral description of the event. Revisit once there's a POV character or title logic to hang it on.
- **POV / connective cast — "why not both."** The Gabby/Greyrose thread is a live option, not a commitment. Gabby's body is Homo Quantus (survives clean) and her mind is an Earth soul (already, literally, "from" the world this all lands on). Per [greyroses.md](iolanu/world-building/organizations/greyroses.md)'s author-level secret, her body's original soul (Laila) isn't destroyed, only suppressed — held under an *ongoing, maintained* function of the Greyrose, not a one-time transfer. The documented failure mode is a death-triggered brief window where the original soul can reassert. A merge-scale quantum shock has no reason to fail that cleanly: rather than a sequential handoff, the mechanism could break into sustained, simultaneous dual occupancy — both souls present and active at once, not one then the other. That's the correct-magnitude failure for this event: nothing else in the merge resolves clean either, so Gabby/Laila becomes a literal, personal-scale instance of the same conflict-marker mess happening to the planet.
  - **Gabby-specific or systemic?** The greyrose mechanism isn't unique to her — every Earth soul on Iolanu (Loisa, Daochen, others) rides the same buffered-original-soul setup. If the shock is big enough to break it once, there's no clean reason it only breaks for her. Making it universal turns this from one character's personal tragedy into a whole inherited class of people: every Earth-soul host on Iolanu waking up double at the same moment, with no precedent for what happened to any of them.
  - **Nobody has a framework for this — not even Nala.** The suppression was an author-level secret; nobody in-world, including the person who built the system, knew failure could look like this. Everything else in the merge eventually gets institutionally processed by someone (the Commission bureaucratizes disasters; there's always a form). This is the one thread that can't be handed to an institution, because no institution knows it exists.
  - **Laila's side is the harder half, and probably the more important one to write.** She believed her sacrifice was total. She reasserts into a body aged however many years without her, on a world she's never heard of, sharing headspace with the person who's been living her unlived life the whole time. Gabby has continuity; Laila is the one experiencing the merge as personal erasure-then-un-erasure — which rhymes directly with what's happening to Iolanu itself: told it didn't matter anymore, still there, waking into a world that moved on without asking.
- **Does Nala personally extract anyone before the merge closes?** Her indifference is to the systemic outcome, not necessarily to individuals she's worked alongside. Unresolved on purpose — determines whether the post-merge Book opens with any face the audience already knows, or entirely on strangers in the wreckage.
- **Scope.** Rifts and Homo Quantus have no reason to respect Earth's Trial being scoped to Manchester specifically ("Manchester didn't ask to speak for the world" — [trial-mechanics.md](earth/trial-mechanics.md)). Whether this event is Manchester-local or planetary is undecided and has large downstream consequences for who witnesses it and how the Commission can possibly manage it.
